# Text Completion (TC): lesson

Text Completion is one of the three official Verbal question families; the exact mix can vary. A one-blank question has five options. Two- and three-blank questions have three options per blank, and **there is no partial credit**: every blank must be right.

Study time: one evening for this lesson, then the three practice sets in `Practice/TC-Practice.md`.

## 1. The method (use it every time)

1. **Cover the options.** They are written to pull you towards wrong answers.
2. **Read the whole sentence** and ask: who or what is the blank describing?
3. **Find the clue.** Every blank has words in the sentence that define it. The GRE never asks for outside knowledge.
4. **Find the direction.** Does the sentence continue in the same direction or turn around? (See the signal words below.)
5. **Write your own simple word** for the blank ("bad", "careful", "not clear"). A simple word is enough.
6. **Now uncover the options** and pick the one that matches your word. Cross out options that go the wrong way.
7. **Re-read the full sentence** with your answers in place. It must make complete logical sense.

## 2. Signal words

| Direction | Words |
|---|---|
| **Turn around** (the blank is the opposite of the clue) | although, though, but, yet, however, despite, in spite of, while, whereas, rather than, instead, nevertheless, nonetheless, paradoxically, ironically, surprisingly, far from, unlike, on the contrary, belies, in contrast |
| **Same direction** (the blank agrees with the clue) | and, also, moreover, indeed, in fact, likewise, similarly, thus, therefore, because, since, so, consequently, as a result, for example, a colon (:), a semicolon (;) |
| **Stronger** (the blank goes further than the clue) | even, not only … but also, so … that, to the point of |
| **Time change** (then vs now) | once, formerly, initially, until recently, no longer, now, at first, eventually |

Watch for **double negatives**: "not uncommon" means common. "Hardly lacking in confidence" means confident. Turn them into plain positive words before you predict.

## 3. Multi-blank questions

- **Start with the easiest blank**, which is often the last one. Each blank you fix becomes a clue for the others.
- The blanks usually depend on each other. If one answer forces an illogical choice in another blank, it's wrong.
- Read the complete text again at the end. One wrong blank loses the whole question.

## 4. Worked examples

**Example 1 (turn around).** *Although the senator was known for her _____ in private meetings, her public speeches were long and rambling.*
Clue: "long and rambling". Signal: "Although", so the blank is the opposite. My word: "short, to the point". The answer would be a word like **concision** or **terseness**.

**Example 2 (same direction, colon).** *The reviewer found the novel _____: its plot was predictable, and its characters were copies of those in a dozen earlier books.*
The colon means the second half explains the blank. Predictable, copies → my word: "unoriginal". Answer: **derivative** (or hackneyed, banal).

**Example 3 (trap of the topic word).** *Far from being _____, the scientist's notebooks show that she tested every claim several times before publishing it.*
"Far from" turns it around. She was very careful, so the blank is "careless". Answer: **slapdash** (or cavalier, lax). A trap option would be "meticulous": it fits the topic, but it goes the wrong way.

**Example 4 (two blanks).** *The committee's report was so (i) _____ that even specialists struggled to follow it; a shorter, (ii) _____ version was released for the public.*
Start with (i): specialists struggled → "hard to understand" → **abstruse**, convoluted. Blank (ii) is the opposite and goes with "shorter" → "clear" → **lucid**, accessible.

**Example 5 (time change).** *Once dismissed as a _____ idea held by a few eccentrics, continental drift is now a foundation of modern geology.*
"Once … now" shows a change. Now it's a foundation, so before it was not respected. My word: "fringe". Answer: **marginal** (or outlandish, heterodox).

## 5. Wrong-answer traps

- **Opposite direction:** the word fits the topic, but you missed "although" or "not".
- **Topic word:** a word related to the subject (a science word in a science sentence) that doesn't match the clue.
- **Too strong or too weak:** the sentence says "slightly worried" and the option means "terrified".
- **The word you don't know:** don't pick an unknown word just because it sounds impressive, and don't reject it for that reason either. Eliminate the words you know are wrong first.

## 6. When you don't know the words

1. Decide if the blank is **positive or negative**. That alone often removes 2–3 options.
2. Use **roots and prefixes** (see `04-Vocab/README.md`): bene- good, mal- bad, a-/an- without, loqu- speak.
3. Guess from the options that remain. **Never leave it blank.**

## 7. Timing

Aim for 1 minute on a one-blank question and up to 1.5–2 minutes on a three-blank question. If you have no prediction after 30 seconds, guess, mark it and move on.
