# Sentence Equivalence (SE): lesson

Sentence Equivalence is one of the three official Verbal question families; the exact mix can vary. Each question has one sentence, one blank and six options. You pick **two** words that each complete the sentence and produce sentences **alike in meaning**. Both must be right; there is no partial credit.

SE questions are the fastest Verbal points on the test (aim for under 1 minute each), and they reward vocabulary more than any other question type. This is why the vocab lists in `04-Vocab` are grouped by meaning.

Study time: one evening for this lesson, then the three sets in `Practice/SE-Practice.md`.

## 1. The method

1. **Cover the options, read the sentence, find the clue and the direction**, exactly as in Text Completion.
2. **Write your own word** for the blank.
3. **Uncover the options and look for pairs.** Among the six words there are usually two or three pairs of near-synonyms.
4. **Pick the pair that matches your word.**
5. **Check both sentences.** Read the sentence once with each word. Do the two sentences say the same thing?

## 2. The two rules that matter

1. **The answer is a pair.** A word that fits the sentence perfectly but has no partner among the options is wrong. (The GRE puts one of these in most questions.)
2. **The pair must fit the sentence.** Two perfect synonyms that don't match the clue are wrong. (The GRE puts one of these pairs in most questions too.)

So the correct answer is where the two rules meet: **a pair that fits.**

Note that the two words don't have to be exact synonyms. They only have to give the sentence the same meaning. "Frugal" and "economical" are fine together even though they are not identical.

## 3. Worked examples

**Example 1.** *The CEO's reply to the shareholders was so _____ that nobody could tell whether the company would cut jobs or not.*
A. candid  B. evasive  C. blunt  D. equivocal  E. lengthy  F. hostile
Clue: "nobody could tell". My word: "unclear on purpose". Pairs: candid/blunt (direct) and evasive/equivocal (avoiding a clear answer). The second pair fits. **B and D.**
The trap: candid/blunt is a real pair, but it means the opposite.

**Example 2.** *Although the diplomat was _____ in public, her private letters were full of sharp opinions about her colleagues.*
A. reticent  B. loquacious  C. reserved  D. gregarious  E. acerbic  F. caustic
"Although" turns it around. Her letters had sharp opinions, so in public she held back. My word: "quiet". **A and C.**
The trap: acerbic/caustic is a pair and matches "sharp", but the blank is the opposite of sharp. Loquacious and gregarious both sound "social", but they aren't a true pair and they go the wrong way.

**Example 3 (a single that fits).** *The new evidence did not disprove the theory, but it did _____ the confidence that many researchers had placed in it.*
A. destroy  B. undermine  C. bolster  D. erode  E. reinforce  F. explain
"Did not disprove … but" means the damage is partial. My word: "weaken". **B and D.**
The trap: "destroy" fits the direction, but it's too strong and has no partner. Bolster/reinforce is a pair going the wrong way.

**Example 4 (a secondary meaning).** *The minister's support for the reform was _____: she backed it only on the condition that taxes would not rise.*
A. qualified  B. unreserved  C. wholehearted  D. conditional  E. belated  F. grudging
The colon explains the blank: "only on the condition". **A and D.** Here "qualified" means limited, not skilled. The GRE loves these second meanings.

## 4. If you're stuck

- Find the pairs first, even before you understand the sentence. Any word with no possible partner can be removed.
- Decide positive or negative. Remove the options with the wrong charge.
- If two pairs are left, guess one pair. That's a 50% chance instead of the 1 in 15 you'd get from guessing blindly.

## 5. Common pairs worth knowing

These pairs come up again and again: laud / extol · disparage / denigrate · assuage / mollify · exacerbate / aggravate · ephemeral / transient · garrulous / loquacious · taciturn / reticent · prodigal / profligate · frugal / parsimonious · candid / forthright · equivocal / ambiguous · obdurate / intransigent · capricious / mercurial · banal / hackneyed · esoteric / arcane · bolster / buttress · undermine / subvert · placate / appease · pragmatic / practical · apathetic / indifferent.
