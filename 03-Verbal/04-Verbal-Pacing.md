# Verbal pacing and elimination strategy

Verbal gives about 90–92 seconds per question **including reading**. The goal is controlled decisions, not equal time on every item.

## 1. Section budgets

| Section | Questions | Time | Useful checkpoints |
|---|---:|---:|---|
| Verbal 1 | 12 | 18 min | After Q4: about 12 min left · after Q8: about 6 min left |
| Verbal 2 | 15 | 23 min | After Q5: about 15 min left · after Q10: about 7–8 min left |

Checkpoint drift of a minute is normal. A three-minute battle with one question is usually not.

## 2. Choose and rehearse one navigation style

Two styles can work:

- **In order:** least navigation cost and no risk of overlooking a question.
- **Text questions first:** TC/SE first, then passages, if those are reliably faster for you.

Test both in folder practice. Keep the style that produces better accuracy with fewer rushed final questions. Do not invent a new order on test day.

## 3. Sentence questions

For Text Completion and Sentence Equivalence:

1. identify the direction word;
2. predict a plain word before choosing;
3. eliminate by logic before nuance;
4. reread the completed sentence.

On SE, a fitting word with no meaning-equivalent partner is not an answer. On multi-blank TC, solve the most constrained blank first. If vocabulary leaves you with no reliable distinction after about 75–90 seconds, make the best remaining choice and move.

## 4. Reading questions

- Spend reading time on structure: viewpoint, turn, purpose and conclusion.
- Return to the relevant line for detail and inference questions.
- For every answer choice, name the defect: **too strong, outside, opposite, true-but-not-asked or mixed-up**.
- Select-all questions are three independent true/false decisions.
- A long passage is not automatically low value; several questions share its reading cost.

## 5. When two choices remain

Do not ask which sounds better. Ask:

1. Which exact word in one choice goes beyond the passage?
2. Whose opinion does each choice describe?
3. Does the question ask what is stated, implied, or the author's purpose?
4. Can I point to textual proof?

If neither choice has proof, return to the passage rather than debating from memory.

## 6. Timing diagnosis

Log slow questions by cause:

- reread the whole passage instead of locating evidence;
- debated two choices without naming the difference;
- lacked vocabulary;
- missed the sentence's direction;
- refused to guess and move;
- changed a correct answer without new evidence.

Your correction should target the cause, not merely demand that you “read faster.”
