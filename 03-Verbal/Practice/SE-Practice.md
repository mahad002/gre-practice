# GRE Verbal Practice: Sentence Equivalence (36 original questions)

- What this is: 36 original Sentence Equivalence questions in three sets of 12 (Set A easy to medium, Set B medium, Set C hard).
- Task: choose exactly TWO of the six options that both fit the sentence and produce sentences alike in meaning. Both must be right to score; there is no partial credit.
- Timing: about 1 minute per question, 1.5 at most. Target time per set: 12 minutes (Set C: allow up to 15).
- Watch for the two classic traps: a synonym pair that does not fit the sentence, and a single word that fits well but has no partner among the options.
- Write your answers on paper, then check the answers section at the bottom of this file. Do not scroll down while solving.

## Answers and explanations

### Set A answers

Quick key: 1 A C, 2 A D, 3 A C, 4 A C, 5 B E, 6 B D, 7 B D, 8 A C, 9 A C, 10 A C, 11 A C, 12 A D

**A1. A, C (tedious, monotonous).** Clues: "Although the subject ... is fascinating" sets up a contrast, and "so _____ that ... struggled to stay awake" gives the result. Trap: *animated* and *lively* are a perfect synonym pair in the wrong direction. Vocab: *erudite* = learned (it would not put students to sleep by itself).

**A2. A, D (plummeted, plunged).** Clues: "had been climbing ... but" requires a reversal, and a cheaper competitor explains a sharp fall. Trap: *soared* and *surged* are synonyms that continue the climb, ignoring "but". *Stabilized* fits the sentence alone (climbing, then flat) but has no partner. Vocab: *fluctuated* = moved up and down.

**A3. A, C (impartiality, fairness).** Clues: "listened to both sides" and "never let personal feelings influence her rulings". Trap: *severity* and *harshness* are a synonym pair associated with judges, but nothing in the sentence is about strict punishment.

**A4. A, C (ambiguous, unclear).** Clues: "so _____ that even experienced technicians could not agree on what they meant". Trap: *detailed* and *thorough* form a pair, but thorough instructions would produce agreement, not confusion. Vocab: *concise* = brief and clear.

**A5. B, E (derelict, dilapidated).** Clues: "once-thriving ... now" signals decline; "warehouses empty and docks rotting" describes a place fallen into ruin. Trap: *bustling* and *thriving* are synonyms that describe the past, not the present. Vocab: *derelict* = abandoned and in ruins; *dilapidated* = fallen into disrepair; *picturesque* = charming to look at.

**A6. B, D (economical, spare).** Clues: "in remarkably few words". Trap: *verbose* and *wordy* are a pair with the opposite meaning. *Lyrical* is a natural compliment for prose and fits alone, but it says nothing about few words and has no partner. Vocab: *spare* = plain, without excess; *ornate* = heavily decorated.

**A7. B, D (transient, short-lived).** Clues: "only a _____ peace" and the semicolon explanation: "within two years ... at war again". Trap: *lasting* and *durable* are a pair in the opposite direction. *Hard-won* is a common phrase with "peace" but clashes with "only" and has no partner. Vocab: *transient* = lasting a short time.

**A8. A, C (aloof, distant).** Clues: "Far from being _____ ... one of the most approachable people". The blank is the opposite of approachable. Trap: *affable* and *friendly* are synonyms of "approachable", so they destroy the contrast. Vocab: *aloof* = cool and remote; *affable* = pleasant, easy to talk to.

**A9. A, C (cautious, guarded).** Clues: "warning that they had not yet been replicated". Trap: *jubilant* and *elated* (both = overjoyed) are a pair that contradicts the warning. *Secretive* looks close to *guarded*, but she is speaking openly, just carefully.

**A10. A, C (circumstantial, indirect).** Clues: "Because ... the jury was reluctant to convict" means the evidence was weak in kind. Trap: *conclusive* and *irrefutable* are synonyms that would make a jury eager to convict. Vocab: *circumstantial* = suggesting a fact without proving it directly; *forensic* = relating to scientific methods of investigating crime.

**A11. A, C (evade, sidestep).** Clues: "rather than confront it" requires the opposite of confronting; postponing "yet again" is avoidance. Trap: *resolve* and *settle* are a pair, but postponing a vote does not settle anything. *Study* fits alone loosely and has no partner.

**A12. A, D (flippant, frivolous).** Clues: "delivered with a grin" and "inappropriate" at a funeral: the remarks lacked seriousness. Trap: *somber* and *solemn* are a pair that suits funerals, so they would not strike anyone as inappropriate. Vocab: *flippant* = not showing proper seriousness.

### Set B answers

Quick key: 1 B D, 2 A C, 3 A C, 4 B D, 5 A C, 6 A C, 7 A C, 8 A C, 9 B D, 10 A C, 11 A C, 12 A C

**B1. B, D (rehash, restatement).** Clues: the colon explains: "the same arguments reappear, supported by the same examples". Trap: *repudiation* and *refutation* are near-synonyms meaning rejection, which is the opposite of repeating. *Refinement* is tempting, but refining implies improvement and the sentence stresses sameness. Vocab: *rehash* = old material presented again with little change; *repudiation* = rejection; *vindication* = proof of being right.

**B2. A, C (ardent, fervent).** Clues: "Even the most _____ supporters ... concede". "Even" needs the extreme case: the strongest supporters. Trap: *lukewarm* and *tepid* are a perfect synonym pair, but weak supporters conceding a flaw is not surprising, so "even" makes no sense. Vocab: *ardent*, *fervent* = passionate; *tepid* = half-hearted.

**B3. A, C (terse, laconic).** Clues: "a single sentence". Trap: *effusive* and *gushing* are a pair meaning overflowing with words or feeling, the opposite. *Belated* is tempting because of "late on a Friday", but that describes the timing in the week, not lateness of the response, and it has no partner. Vocab: *terse*, *laconic* = using few words; *contrite* = sincerely sorry.

**B4. B, D (irreconcilable, incompatible).** Clues: "Although ... often presented as _____, ... each can accommodate the other's central claims". The common presentation is the opposite of "can accommodate". Trap: *compatible* and *complementary* agree with the second clause, leaving "although" with no contrast. Vocab: *irreconcilable* = impossible to bring into agreement; *obsolete* = out of date.

**B5. A, C (isolated, secluded).** Clues: the village "preserved" old dialect features that "vanished elsewhere", which is what happens when a place is cut off from outside contact. Trap: *imperiled* and *endangered* are synonyms, but being in danger does not explain preservation. *Impoverished* fits a mountain village alone, but has no partner and no link to dialect. Vocab: *secluded* = shut off from others; *imperiled* = put in danger.

**B6. A, C (contrived, artificial).** Clues: the colon explains: resolved "by a coincidence that no viewer could have accepted". Trap: *poignant* and *moving* are a pair that praises the ending, but the sentence criticizes it. *Abrupt* could describe such an ending but has no partner, and the complaint is implausibility, not speed. Vocab: *contrived* = obviously forced, not natural; *poignant* = touching.

**B7. A, C (duplicity, deceitfulness).** Clues: "privately promised her vote to both sides" is double-dealing. Trap: *naivety* and *credulity* are a pair, but they describe someone easily fooled, not someone fooling others. *Indecision* is tempting, but making two secret promises is deception, not hesitation. Vocab: *duplicity* = deliberate deceit, double-dealing; *credulity* = readiness to believe anything.

**B8. A, C (serendipitous, fortuitous).** Clues: "What the documentary presents as ... was in fact ... decades of deliberate, incremental research". The blank is the opposite of deliberate: a lucky accident. Trap: *painstaking* and *laborious* are a pair that agrees with "deliberate", so "in fact" would have nothing to correct. *Momentous* fits alone and has no partner. Vocab: *serendipitous*, *fortuitous* = happening by lucky chance.

**B9. B, D (convoluted, byzantine).** Clues: "intended to simplify ... have instead". The result is the opposite of simple. Trap: *streamlined* and *efficient* are a pair that matches the intention, not the outcome. Vocab: *convoluted* = extremely complicated; *byzantine* = excessively intricate and hard to navigate.

**B10. A, C (exacting, stringent).** Clues: the colon: she returned any paper with "even a single unsupported claim". Trap: *lax* and *lenient* are a pair with the opposite meaning. *Arbitrary* is wrong because her rule is consistent and has a reason. Vocab: *exacting* = demanding great care; *stringent* = strict.

**B11. A, C (humble, modest).** Clues: "Despite ... beginnings in a rented garage ... now employs more than ten thousand". The beginnings were small. Trap: *auspicious* and *promising* are a pair, but "despite promising beginnings" would need a disappointing outcome. Vocab: *auspicious* = suggesting future success.

**B12. A, C (accessible, approachable).** Clues: "assumes no prior knowledge and explains each technical term". Trap: *recondite* and *esoteric* are a pair meaning understood only by specialists, the opposite. *Scholarly* and *comprehensive* each sound right for a catalog essay, but neither is what the colon explains, and neither has a partner. Vocab: *recondite* = obscure, little known; *esoteric* = meant for a small group with special knowledge.

### Set C answers

Quick key: 1 A E, 2 A E, 3 A C, 4 A C, 5 A C, 6 A D, 7 A D, 8 A D, 9 A C, 10 A C, 11 A C, 12 A C

**C1. A, E (evasive, equivocal).** Clues: the colon: it "acknowledged that mistakes had been made without specifying what they were or who had made them". The apology avoids committing to anything. Trap: *heartfelt* and *sincere* are a pair in the wrong direction. *Abject* (utterly humble) is a common partner of "apology" but the wrong meaning. *Grudging* is negative and tempting, but the fault described is vagueness, not unwillingness, and it has no partner. Vocab: *equivocal* = deliberately vague or ambiguous.

**C2. A, E (specious, fallacious).** Clues: "though ingenious, is ultimately _____" needs a fault; it "appears to prove" its conclusion only by assuming it. An argument that looks sound but is not. Trap: *cogent* and *compelling* are a pair of praise words that ignore "though". *Abstruse* (hard to follow) is a fault, but not the one described. Vocab: *specious* = seeming true but actually false; *fallacious* = based on faulty reasoning; *cogent* = convincing; *prolix* = too wordy.

**C3. A, C (intransigent, obdurate).** Clues: "Having expected ... were disconcerted by her readiness to concede". The expectation was the opposite of conceding: stubbornness. Trap: *pliant* and *tractable* are a pair meaning easily persuaded, which is what she turned out to be, not what was expected. Vocab: *intransigent* = refusing to compromise; *obdurate* = stubbornly unyielding; *tractable* = easy to manage; *urbane* = polished and courteous.

**C4. A, C (exalt, glorify).** Clues: she "resists the temptation" and records "pettiness and vanity as scrupulously as his achievements". The temptation is to show only the good side. Trap: *disparage* and *denigrate* are a pair, but a biographer tempted to belittle would not be resisting by recording faults. *Scrutinize* fits "scrupulously" by association, yet she does scrutinize him, so it is not something she resists. Vocab: *denigrate* = unfairly criticize; *emulate* = imitate.

**C5. A, C (punctilious, scrupulous).** Clues: "to a fault" means a virtue carried too far, and the colon shows which virtue: no claim passes without qualification and footnotes, which is extreme care about accuracy. Trap: *slipshod* and *slapdash* are a pair meaning careless, the opposite. *Prolix* (wordy) fits the result quite well, but "to a fault" requires a quality that is normally good, and it has no partner. Vocab: *punctilious* = attentive to every detail of correctness; *derivative* = unoriginal.

**C6. A, D (premature, precipitate).** Clues: the semicolon: a decade after it was "written off", it "still underlies" most transactions. The reports came too soon. Trap: *prescient* and *prophetic* are a pair meaning correctly foreseeing, the opposite of what happened. Vocab: *precipitate* (adjective) = hasty, done too soon; *demise* = death, end.

**C7. A, D (nebulous, indeterminate).** Clues: "so _____ that officials on opposite sides ... have each cited them". Guidelines that support both sides are vague. Trap: two synonym pairs here. *Explicit* and *unequivocal* (clear) are the opposite; *draconian* and *stringent* (harsh, strict) are about severity, which does not explain both sides claiming support. Vocab: *nebulous* = vague, cloudy; *draconian* = excessively harsh.

**C8. A, D (genial, cordial).** Clues: "hard to square with" a reputation for misanthropy, plus "and unfailingly generous": the blank is a warm, friendly quality. Trap: *caustic* and *acerbic* are a pair that matches the reputation, not the letters. *Prolific* suits a correspondent but does not contradict misanthropy and has no partner. Vocab: *misanthropy* = dislike of people; *caustic*, *acerbic* = sharply sarcastic.

**C9. A, C (opportunistic, self-serving).** Clues: "Skeptics" and the timing: it "coincided exactly with" an inquiry into the firm's emissions. They think the enthusiasm serves the firm's interests. Trap: *altruistic* and *disinterested* are a pair (disinterested means unbiased, having no personal stake, not bored), and they are the opposite of what skeptics think. *Belated* fits alone but has no partner. Vocab: *quixotic* = idealistic and impractical.

**C10. A, C (tenuous, slender).** Clues: "while suggestive" concedes a little; "too _____ to justify the sweeping policy changes" needs "weak, thin". Trap: *substantial* and *copious* are a pair meaning plentiful, the opposite. *Recent* could fit alone (evidence too new) but has no partner. Vocab: *tenuous* = thin, weak; *copious* = abundant.

**C11. A, C (insouciance, nonchalance).** Clues: "Although he affected an air of _____" contrasts with "how anxiously he monitored every review". He pretended not to care. Trap: *diligence* and *assiduity* are a pair, but hard work is consistent with anxious monitoring, so there is no contrast. Vocab: *affected* = pretended to have; *insouciance*, *nonchalance* = casual lack of concern; *assiduity* = persistent hard work.

**C12. A, C (belied, undercut).** Clues: the claim is "inevitable"; her own evidence shows "how narrowly the measure passed and how easily it might have failed". The evidence contradicts the claim. Trap: *buttressed* and *bolstered* are a pair meaning supported, the opposite direction. *Complicated* fits alone, but it is weaker than the two answers and has no partner. Vocab: *belied* = shown to be false; *buttressed* = supported.

## Diagnose your result

Do not convert an original set into a GRE scaled score.

- **11–12 correct:** strong control of this set; review any guess or slow item.
- **9–10 correct:** review the exact clue and the rejected synonym pairs, then continue.
- **8 or fewer:** rebuild the missing vocabulary/logic and redo the set later.

For every miss, note whether you chose a pair that did not fit the sentence, or one fitting word plus a non-equivalent partner. Use official timed POWERPREP results for score decisions.
