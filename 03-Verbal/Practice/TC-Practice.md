# GRE Verbal Practice: Text Completion (36 original questions)

- What this is: 36 original Text Completion questions in three sets of 12 (Set A easy to medium, Set B medium, Set C hard). Each set has 5 one-blank, 4 two-blank and 3 three-blank questions.
- Timing: about 1 to 1.5 minutes per question. Target time per set: 15 minutes (Set C: allow up to 17).
- Scoring rule, same as the real test: a two-blank or three-blank question counts as correct only if every blank is correct. No partial credit.
- Method: read the whole sentence, find the signal words (although, yet, far from, indeed, colon, semicolon), predict your own word, then look at the options.
- Write your answers on paper, then check the answers section at the bottom of this file. Do not scroll down while solving.

## Answers and explanations

### Set A answers

Quick key: 1 B, 2 A, 3 C, 4 B, 5 C, 6 A E, 7 A E, 8 A E, 9 B D, 10 A E H, 11 A E H, 12 B D H

**A1. B (muted).** Clues: "Although ... expected ... heated debate" sets up a contrast, and the colon explains it: "passed with scarcely a comment". You need "quiet". Trap: *acrimonious* (bitter, angry) matches "heated debate", which is the opposite direction. Vocab: *partisan* = strongly one-sided; *erratic* = unpredictable.

**A2. A (meticulous).** Clues: "so _____ that" gives a result: others can reconstruct behavior "almost hour by hour". Only extremely careful, detailed notes allow that. Trap: *terse* (brief) and *cursory* (hasty, superficial) both describe notes but would make reconstruction impossible. Vocab: *idiosyncratic* = peculiar to one person.

**A3. C (reclusive).** Clues: "Far from being" signals the opposite of what follows: "a wide circle of friends" and many correspondents. The opposite is someone who avoids company. Trap: *gregarious* (sociable) describes what he actually was, not the false image. Vocab: *prolific* = producing a great deal; *eminent* = famous and respected.

**A4. B (ductile).** Clues: "Because ... it can be drawn into wires ... without breaking". *Ductile* means exactly that: able to be stretched into wire. Trap: *brittle* is a metals word but means it breaks easily, the opposite. Vocab: *volatile* = evaporating easily, or unstable.

**A5. C (brevity).** Clues: the colon explains the blank: "in barely five minutes she had finished". Trap: *eloquence* is the quality people usually praise in speeches, but nothing after the colon is about beautiful language. Vocab: *candor* = frankness; *vehemence* = forcefulness.

**A6. A, E (dismissive; soared).** Clues: "but ... since" requires a change of direction, and the colon tells you where it ended: "among the finest works". So reviews began badly and the reputation rose. Trap: *effusive* (gushing with praise) with *languished* also makes a contrast, but it contradicts "now routinely listed among the finest". Vocab: *languished* = weakened, was neglected.

**A7. A, E (bolster; undermined).** Clues: "although intended to ... had in practice" requires opposite effects, and "encouraging large agribusinesses to ... dominate" tells you the real effect was harmful. A subsidy is intended to help. Trap: *displace* is what happened to the farmers, but it belongs to the result, not the intention; it leaves no contrast. Vocab: *bolster* = support; *emboldened* = made braver.

**A8. A, E (barren; teems with).** Clues: "once regarded ... but ... have revealed" plus the list of living things. So the old view was "lifeless" and the new view is "full of life". Trap: *is hostile to* fits the old view, not what the expeditions revealed. Vocab: *barren* = lifeless, unproductive; *teems with* = is full of.

**A9. B, D (autocratic; dissent).** Clues: the colon defines the style: "rarely explained her decisions and expected them to be carried out". That is ruling by command, without disagreement. Trap: *haphazard* (random, unplanned) is a negative management word but nothing suggests disorder. Vocab: *autocratic* = ruling alone, like a dictator; *dissent* = disagreement.

**A10. A, E, H (neglected; unfounded; intact).** Clues: scholars "assumed that it had been thoroughly looted", so they ignored it. "However" reverses the assumption, and "contents undisturbed" defines the last blank. Trap: *ransacked* and *empty* agree with the old assumption, but "however" and "undisturbed" rule them out. Vocab: *prescient* = showing foresight; *intact* = whole, undamaged; *unfounded* = without basis.

**A11. A, E, H (encouraging; overstate; warrant).** Clues: "Although ... careful not to" means the results were good but the researchers stayed modest. "Too small to _____ firm conclusions" needs "justify". Trap: *ambiguous* results would not need the "although"; there would be nothing to overstate. Vocab: *warrant* = justify; *preclude* = prevent.

**A12. B, D, H (resounding; short-lived; overwhelmed).** Clues: the colon ("ridership exceeded forecasts within months") shows a clear, quick success. "Yet" turns the triumph sour, and "construction halted" shows the budget could not cope. Trap: *qualified* success (a limited one) is a common GRE phrase, but the first sentence gives no limitation, and "within months" rules out *belated*. Vocab: *resounding* = emphatic, unmistakable.

### Set B answers

Quick key: 1 B, 2 B, 3 A, 4 A, 5 B, 6 B D, 7 A E, 8 A E, 9 B D, 10 A E H, 11 A E H, 12 A E G

**B1. B (descriptive).** Clues: "though rich in ... detail" concedes a strength; the colon states the weakness: it "catalogs events" but "never ventures an explanation". It describes without explaining. Trap: *tendentious* and *polemical* both mean pushing an argument, which is the opposite of an account that argues nothing. Vocab: *tendentious* = biased toward a point of view; *polemical* = aggressively argumentative; *revisionist* = challenging the accepted view; *apocryphal* = of doubtful authenticity.

**B2. B (perverse).** Clues: "might have seemed _____; in fact, it proved to be shrewd". The blank must contrast with "shrewd", and the opening clause explains why: reissuing old designs runs against an industry "that prizes novelty". *Perverse* = deliberately contrary to what is expected or sensible. Trap: *astute* means the same as "shrewd", so it destroys the contrast. Vocab: *opportunistic* = exploiting circumstances for gain.

**B3. A (laconic).** Clues: "usually _____ ... surprised reporters by speaking at length". The usual behavior is the opposite of speaking at length. Trap: *voluble* (talkative) is the same direction as "at length", so there would be no surprise. Vocab: *laconic* = using very few words; *irascible* = easily angered; *genial* = friendly.

**B4. A (fragmentary).** Clues: "Because ... so _____ ... must remain provisional". Something about the record makes firm claims impossible: it is incomplete. Trap: *ancient* is associated with fossils, but age alone does not make conclusions provisional. Vocab: *voluminous* = very large in quantity; *provisional* = temporary, subject to revision.

**B5. B (extravagant).** Clues: "so _____ that ... suspected it of being ironic". Only excessive praise sounds like mockery. Trap: *grudging* or *perfunctory* praise would seem unenthusiastic, not ironic. Vocab: *perfunctory* = done with minimal effort; *measured* = restrained, moderate.

**B6. B, D (popular; unsupported).** Clues: "elegance has made it _____ ... yet elegance is no guarantee of truth". Elegance attracts, so the first blank is positive; the colon then gives the reason for doubt: predictions lack experimental backing. Trap: *suspect* fits the doubtful mood of the second half but leaves "yet" with nothing to contrast. Vocab: *suspect* (adjective) = doubtful, not to be trusted.

**B7. A, E (cryptic; explicit).** Clues: "Unlike her predecessor" requires opposites; "left investors guessing" defines the first blank as hard to interpret. Trap: *evasive* matches the predecessor, not the new governor. Vocab: *cryptic* = mysterious, hard to interpret; *forthright* = direct and open.

**B8. A, E (sterility; profusion).** Clues: "apparent ... is deceptive" means the desert only looks lifeless; the semicolon explains that life is waiting ("dormant seeds", "burrowing animals") and the rains "transform" it. The second blank is the opposite of lifelessness: abundance. Trap: *aridity* (dryness) is true of deserts but cannot be what rain produces. Vocab: *profusion* = great abundance.

**B9. B, D (faithful; an apologia).** Clues: "purports to be" signals a claim that is not fully true; "omitting episodes that reflect poorly on him" shows the book really defends its author. Trap: *a chronicle* is a neutral record, which is what the book pretends to be, not what it actually is. Vocab: *purports* = claims; *apologia* = a formal defense of one's own conduct; *elegy* = a poem or text of mourning.

**B10. A, E, H (checkered; discredited; tempered).** Clues: the story has ups and downs: "embraced enthusiastically", then "support failed to materialize", then a version that says language "merely influences" has "regained respectability". Trap: *radical* for blank (iii) contradicts "merely influences", which is a weaker claim. Vocab: *checkered* = marked by alternating good and bad periods; *vindicated* = proved right; *tempered* = moderated.

**B11. A, E, H (alleviate; entices; severe).** Clues: planners expect widening to help. "Suggest the opposite" plus "so that ... congestion is ... as _____ as before" means new capacity pulls in new drivers and congestion returns. Trap: *deters* is the opposite direction: if capacity deterred drivers, congestion would fall. Vocab: *alleviate* = make less severe; *induced demand* = extra use created by extra supply.

**B12. A, E, G (feeble; incapacity; economy).** Clues: "once dismissed as ... efforts of a failing talent" needs a negative word; "now seen as deliberately spare" and "every omitted detail was a considered choice" show the sparseness was skill. So critics took it for inability, and it was in fact economy of means. Trap: *restraint* for (ii) leaves no contrast with (iii): restraint and economy are both deliberate virtues. Vocab: *economy* (in art) = using only what is needed.

### Set C answers

Quick key: 1 A, 2 B, 3 B, 4 C, 5 A, 6 A D, 7 B D, 8 A D, 9 A D, 10 A E G, 11 B E G, 12 B E G

**C1. A (circumspection).** Clues: the colon defines the blank: "without once committing its author to a judgment that any party could find objectionable". That is extreme caution about what one says. Trap: *candor* (frankness) is what memoirs are usually praised for, but this one avoids frank judgments altogether. Vocab: *circumspection* = wary caution; *erudition* = deep learning; *concision* = brevity.

**C2. B (vitiate).** Clues: "does not, in itself, _____ their conclusions, though it does oblige readers to scrutinize". The funding is a reason for suspicion, but the sentence says it does not go as far as invalidating the results. Trap: *corroborate* (confirm) reverses the logic: no one would think industry funding confirms a study. Vocab: *vitiate* = spoil or invalidate; *elucidate* = make clear; *presage* = foreshadow; *recapitulate* = summarize.

**C3. B (facile).** Clues: "often praised as limpid, is in fact merely _____" asks for a negative version of clear and simple; the colon specifies: simplicity with nothing underneath. Trap: *lapidary* (polished and concise) is a prose-style word, but it is praise, and "merely" needs a fault. *Turgid* and *abstruse* contradict "surface simplicity". Vocab: *limpid* = clear; *facile* = superficially easy, shallow; *turgid* = swollen, pompous; *abstruse* = hard to understand; *mordant* = bitingly sarcastic.

**C4. C (exhaustion).** Clues: "overlook how much it owed to _____" contrasts with "triumph of statesmanship", and the colon explains: each side "lacked the means to continue the war". The peace came from being worn out, not from wisdom. Trap: *foresight* and *magnanimity* are statesmanlike virtues, exactly what the sentence denies. Vocab: *magnanimity* = generosity toward a rival; *duplicity* = deceit; *intransigence* = refusal to compromise.

**C5. A (severity).** Clues: "not wholly deserved: although his public buildings are indeed austere". "Indeed" confirms that the reputation is for austerity; the private houses "abound in ornament and playful detail", which is the exception. Trap: *whimsy* describes the private houses, not the reputation. Vocab: *severity* (of style) = plainness, strictness; *eclecticism* = drawing on many styles; *probity* = moral integrity.

**C6. A, D (inaccurate; anodyne).** Clues: "not that the biography is _____, for its scholarship is impeccable" means the first blank is a fault that impeccable scholarship excludes. The second: trying "to be fair to every faction" produced a portrait in which an "abrasive personality never comes into focus", so it is bland. Trap: *tendentious* (biased) is the opposite of being fair to every faction. Vocab: *anodyne* = inoffensive to the point of blandness; *impeccable* = faultless.

**C7. B, D (transitory; disingenuous).** Clues: after the colon, the costs "will recur annually and are projected to grow". That refutes a claim that the costs are temporary, and a claim made by "proponents" that conflicts with known facts is not candid. Trap: *unavoidable* sounds like something proponents would say, but recurring costs do not show that a cost is avoidable. Vocab: *transitory* = temporary; *disingenuous* = not sincere, pretending to know less than one does; *prohibitive* = too high to pay.

**C8. A, D (strengthened; elasticity).** Clues: "is not thereby _____; on the contrary" plus "deprives it of explanatory power" means fitting everything looks like a strength but is a weakness. "Its very _____" names the property of stretching to fit any observation. Trap: *precision* is a scientific virtue, but a claim "that forbids nothing" is the opposite of precise. Vocab: *elasticity* = ability to stretch.

**C9. A, D (obscurity; commonplace).** Clues: "mistook _____ for profundity" means the poems seemed deep only because they were hard ("difficulties of her syntax"). Once worked through, the thoughts turn out to be ordinary. Trap: *intricate* or *subversive* would make the thoughts genuinely impressive, which contradicts "mistook". Vocab: *commonplace* = ordinary, unoriginal; *lucidity* = clarity.

**C10. A, E, G (inimical; transmitted; trust).** Clues: "It would be a mistake to regard ... as simply _____" followed by "did restrict ... but they also" means the common view is negative and the sentence adds positives: passing on knowledge "through apprenticeship" and giving buyers confidence in goods "they could not inspect". Trap: *hoarded* fits the negative stereotype of guilds but cannot follow "but they also". Vocab: *inimical* = hostile, harmful; *conducive* = helpful to.

**C11. B, E, G (an unapologetic; disparaged; opacity).** Clues: the colon shows she believed firmly in explaining simply, so she popularized without embarrassment. Colleagues who saw it "as a distraction" looked down on it, and she thought they confused being hard to understand with being rigorous. Trap: *a reluctant* popularizer is a familiar phrase, but her stated principle shows conviction, not reluctance. Vocab: *disparaged* = belittled; *opacity* = lack of clarity.

**C12. B, E, G (chary; significant; sufficient).** Clues: historians distrust single-cause accounts. "Surely _____, yet" concedes that coal mattered; "regions equally rich in coal failed to industrialize" shows that having coal was not enough by itself. Trap: *necessary* for (iii): the evidence (coal without industry) tests sufficiency, not necessity. Vocab: *chary* = cautious, wary; *enamored* = fond; *heedless* = careless of.
