# Word Problems

Word problems test whether you can turn English into equations quickly. The maths is usually one linear equation. The difficulty is the setup, and a handful of standard templates (rates, work, mixtures, sets) cover nearly all of them.
Study plan (about 2 days): day 1 = read sections 1 to 3 and do practice 1 to 10. Day 2 = practice 11 to 25, then review every problem you missed or guessed.

## 1. What you must know

**Translating words into equations**

1. "is", "was", "equals" mean =. "of" means multiply. "per" means divide. "what" is the unknown. Example: "What is 30% of 50?" becomes x = 0.3 * 50.
2. "5 less than x" is x - 5, not 5 - x. "x is 5 more than y" is x = y + 5. Example: "3 more than twice a number is 19" becomes 2n + 3 = 19.
3. "A is twice as old as B" is A = 2B. Test with numbers if unsure: the bigger quantity needs no multiplier. Example: "There are 3 times as many cats as dogs" is c = 3d.
4. Name the variable for what the question asks, and write the units next to it. At the end, check that you answered the question asked (the son's age, not the mother's).
5. If the options are numbers, back-solving is often faster than algebra. If the problem has no real amounts, only fractions or percents, choose your own total (100, or a common multiple of the denominators).

**Age problems**

6. Make a small table: rows are people, columns are "now" and "then". In t years everyone is t years older. Example: Sara is 4s now and her son is s. In 20 years: 4s + 20 and s + 20.
7. The age difference between two people never changes. Example: if A is 5 years older than B now, A was 5 years older six years ago too.

**Consecutive numbers**

8. Consecutive integers: n, n + 1, n + 2. Consecutive even or odd integers: n, n + 2, n + 4. Example: three consecutive even integers that sum to 78: 3n + 6 = 78, so n = 24.
9. For an odd count of evenly spaced numbers, the middle one is the average. Example: five consecutive integers with sum 365 have middle value 73.

**Rate, time, distance**

10. Distance = rate * time. Also rate = distance/time and time = distance/rate. Example: 150 miles in 2.5 hours is 60 miles per hour.
11. Average speed = total distance / total time. It is never the plain average of the speeds unless the times are equal. Example: 60 km at 30 km/h (2 h) then 60 km at 60 km/h (1 h): 120/3 = 40 km/h, not 45.
12. Equal distances at speeds a and b: average speed = 2ab/(a + b). It is always below the plain average. Example: 40 and 60 give 4,800/100 = 48.
13. Moving toward each other: add the speeds (closing speed). Example: 420 km apart at 80 and 60 km/h, they meet after 420/140 = 3 hours.
14. Catching up (same direction): subtract the speeds. Time = head start distance / difference in speeds. Example: a 24 km head start, speeds 12 and 20: 24/8 = 3 hours.
15. Round trips: the distance out equals the distance back. Write time out + time back = total time. Example: d/40 + d/60 = 5 gives d = 120.
16. Currents and wind: downstream speed = boat + current, upstream speed = boat - current.
17. Keep units consistent. 30 minutes is 0.5 hours. To change km/h into m/s divide by 3.6. Example: 90 km/h = 25 m/s.

**Work and combined rates**

18. Rate = 1 job / time. If a job takes 6 hours, the rate is 1/6 of the job per hour.
19. Rates add. Times do not. Together: 1/a + 1/b = 1/t. Example: 6 hours and 3 hours: 1/6 + 1/3 = 1/2, so 2 hours together.
20. Shortcut for two workers: t = ab/(a + b). Example: 4 and 6 hours give 24/10 = 2.4 hours.
21. The combined time is always less than the fastest worker's time alone. Use this to remove wrong options.
22. A drain or leak is a negative rate. Example: fill in 12 h, fill in 6 h, drain in 8 h: 1/12 + 1/6 - 1/8 = 1/8, so 8 hours.
23. Work = rate * time also covers partial jobs. Example: a worker with rate 1/30 working 20 hours does 2/3 of the job.
24. Identical workers: work = workers * rate * time. Example: 8 workers for 15 days = 120 worker-days, so 12 workers need 10 days.

**Mixtures and alloys**

25. Track the pure ingredient: amount of ingredient = concentration * total amount. Example: 10 litres of 30% acid contains 3 litres of acid.
26. Mixing: (c1 * amount1 + c2 * amount2) / (amount1 + amount2) = final concentration. Example: 10 L of 30% plus 15 L of 50%: (3 + 7.5)/25 = 42%.
27. Adding pure water changes the total but not the amount of ingredient. Evaporation does the same in reverse. Example: 20 L of 40% salt has 8 L of salt. To reach 25%: 8/(20 + w) = 0.25, so w = 12.
28. Pure ingredient is 100% and water is 0%.
29. The mixture's concentration is always between the two starting concentrations, and closer to the one with more volume.

**Weighted averages**

30. Weighted average = (sum of value * weight) / (sum of weights). Example: 20 students averaging 80 and 30 averaging 90: (1,600 + 2,700)/50 = 86.
31. Balance (see-saw) rule: the weights are in the inverse ratio of the distances from the average. Example: $6 and $10 nuts mixed to $7. Distances are 1 and 3, so the amounts are in ratio 3 to 1 (more of the $6 nuts).
32. Sum = average * count. Most average problems are solved by working with sums. Example: if 4 numbers average 10 and a 5th makes the average 12, the 5th is 60 - 40 = 20.
33. Without the group sizes (or their ratio) you cannot find a combined average. You only know that it lies between the group averages. This is a common QC answer of D.

**Profit, loss, discount, markup**

34. Profit = selling price - cost. Profit percent is on cost unless the question says otherwise. Example: cost 40, price 50: profit is 10/40 = 25% of cost, but 10/50 = 20% of the selling price.
35. Markup is a percent of cost. Discount is a percent of the marked (list) price. Example: cost 80, marked up 50% to 120, then 30% off: 84. Profit 4.
36. Successive discounts multiply. Example: 20% off then 10% off = 0.8 * 0.9 = 0.72, which is 28% off.
37. To find the original price, divide by the multiplier. Example: sale price 90 after 25% off: original = 90/0.75 = 120.
38. Revenue = price * quantity. Total profit = revenue - total cost.

**Overlapping sets**

39. Two sets: Total = A + B - both + neither. Example: 50 people, 30 like tea, 25 like coffee, 10 like both: neither = 50 - (30 + 25 - 10) = 5.
40. A 2x2 table handles every two-set problem. Rows: in A, not in A. Columns: in B, not in B. Fill what is given and complete rows and columns by subtraction.
41. Limits on the overlap: "both" is at most the smaller group, and at least A + B - Total (or 0 if that is negative). Example: Total 40, A = 25, B = 22: both is between 7 and 22.
42. Three sets: Total = A + B + C - (A and B) - (A and C) - (B and C) + (all three) + none. Here each pair count includes the people in all three.
43. "Exactly two" form: Total = A + B + C - (exactly two) - 2 * (all three) + none. Read carefully which pair counts the question gives.

**Unit conversions**

44. Multiply by fractions equal to 1 so the units cancel. Example: 90 km/h = 90 * (1,000 m / 1 km) * (1 h / 3,600 s) = 25 m/s.
45. Facts to know: 1 hour = 60 minutes = 3,600 seconds. 1 km = 1,000 m. 1 m = 100 cm. 1 kg = 1,000 g. 1 foot = 12 inches. 1 yard = 3 feet. Other facts (miles, pounds, gallons) are given in the question when needed.
46. Area and volume conversions use the factor squared or cubed. Example: 1 square yard = 9 square feet. 1 cubic metre = 1,000,000 cubic centimetres.

## 2. Worked examples

**Example 1 (Multiple choice, one answer)**
Ali is 5 years older than Bina. Six years ago, Ali was twice as old as Bina. How old is Ali now?
(A) 11 (B) 12 (C) 16 (D) 17 (E) 22

Solution: Let Bina be b now, so Ali is b + 5. Six years ago: b + 5 - 6 = 2(b - 6). So b - 1 = 2b - 12 and b = 11. Ali is 16. Check: six years ago they were 10 and 5.
Fastest method: back-solve. Try C: Ali 16, Bina 11, six years ago 10 and 5. It works.
The trap: 11 is Bina's age. Answer the question asked.
Answer: C.

**Example 2 (Multiple choice, one answer)**
A cyclist rides 60 km at 30 km/h and then another 60 km at 60 km/h. What is the cyclist's average speed for the whole ride, in km/h?
(A) 36 (B) 40 (C) 42 (D) 45 (E) 48

Solution: Time for the first part: 60/30 = 2 hours. Second part: 60/60 = 1 hour. Total: 120 km in 3 hours = 40 km/h.
The trap: 45, the plain average of 30 and 60. More time is spent at the slower speed, so the average is pulled below 45.
Answer: B.

**Example 3 (Numeric entry)**
Machine A can complete a job in 4 hours. Machine B can complete the same job in 6 hours. Working together at these rates, how many hours do they take to complete the job? Give the answer as a decimal.

Solution: Rates: 1/4 + 1/6 = 3/12 + 2/12 = 5/12 of the job per hour. Time = 12/5 = 2.4 hours.
The trap: averaging the times (5 hours) or adding them (10 hours). Together must be faster than the faster machine alone, so the answer must be under 4.
Answer: 2.4.

**Example 4 (Quantitative Comparison)**
A shop buys a lamp for $80. It marks the price up by 50% and then sells the lamp at a 30% discount off the marked price.
Quantity A: The shop's profit on the lamp, in dollars
Quantity B: 5

Solution: Marked price: 80 * 1.5 = 120. Sale price: 120 * 0.7 = 84. Profit: 84 - 80 = 4. That is less than 5.
The trap: 50% - 30% = 20%, so "profit is 16 dollars". The 30% is taken from 120, not from 80.
Answer: B.

**Example 5 (Multiple choice, one answer)**
How many litres of a 60% acid solution must be added to 30 litres of a 20% acid solution to produce a 30% acid solution?
(A) 5 (B) 7.5 (C) 10 (D) 12 (E) 15

Solution: Track the acid. 0.2 * 30 + 0.6x = 0.3(30 + x). So 6 + 0.6x = 9 + 0.3x, 0.3x = 3, x = 10.
Fastest method: balance rule. 30% is 10 away from 20% and 30 away from 60%. So the volumes are in ratio 30 to 10: three parts of the 20% (30 litres) to one part of the 60% (10 litres).
The trap: forgetting that the total volume also grows by x.
Answer: C.

**Example 6 (Select all that apply)**
In a survey of 200 people, 120 are women. A total of 90 people like jazz, and 50 of the men like jazz. Which of the following statements are true?
(A) 40 women like jazz.
(B) 30 men do not like jazz.
(C) 80 women do not like jazz.
(D) More than half of the women like jazz.
(E) 110 people do not like jazz.

Solution: Build the 2x2 table. Men = 200 - 120 = 80. Men who like jazz = 50, so men who do not = 30. Women who like jazz = 90 - 50 = 40, so women who do not = 120 - 40 = 80. People who do not like jazz = 30 + 80 = 110. For (D): 40 out of 120 women is one third, so it is false.

| | Jazz | Not jazz | Total |
|---|---|---|---|
| Women | 40 | 80 | 120 |
| Men | 50 | 30 | 80 |
| Total | 90 | 110 | 200 |

The trap: mixing up "50 men like jazz" with "50 people like jazz".
Answer: A, B, C and E.

**Example 7 (Quantitative Comparison)**
A grocer mixes nuts that cost $6 per pound with nuts that cost $10 per pound. The mixture costs $7 per pound.
Quantity A: The number of pounds of the $6 nuts in the mixture
Quantity B: The number of pounds of the $10 nuts in the mixture

Solution: The mixture price 7 is closer to 6 than to 10, so there is more of the $6 kind. Exactly: 6a + 10b = 7(a + b) gives 3b = a, so a is three times b. The actual weights are unknown, but a > b always.
The trap: choosing D because no weights are given. The ratio is enough for a comparison.
Answer: A.

**Example 8 (Multiple choice, one answer)**
A cyclist leaves a town riding at 12 km/h. Two hours later, a second cyclist leaves the same town on the same road riding at 20 km/h. How many hours after the second cyclist leaves does she catch up with the first?
(A) 1.2 (B) 2 (C) 3 (D) 4 (E) 5

Solution: Head start: 12 * 2 = 24 km. The gap closes at 20 - 12 = 8 km/h. Time = 24/8 = 3 hours. Check: the first has ridden 5 hours * 12 = 60 km, the second 3 hours * 20 = 60 km.
The trap: 5 hours is the first cyclist's riding time. The question asks for the second cyclist's time.
Answer: C.

## 3. Common traps

- Averaging speeds. Average speed is total distance over total time.
- Adding times in work problems. Add rates, then flip.
- Answering for the wrong variable (Bina's age instead of Ali's, the first cyclist's time instead of the second's).
- "5 less than x" written as 5 - x. "A is 20% more than B" written as B = 1.2A.
- Taking profit percent on the selling price when the question means cost (or the reverse). Read which base is stated.
- Subtracting percents for markup then discount. Multiply the multipliers.
- Averaging two percents or two averages without the group sizes.
- In set problems, counting the overlap twice. Subtract "both" once in the two-set formula.
- In three-set problems, confusing "in A and B" (includes all three) with "in exactly A and B only".
- Mixed units: minutes with hours, km/h with metres per second. Convert before you calculate.

## 5. Answers and solutions

**Answer key:** 1 B, 2 B, 3 D, 4 10, 5 C, 6 E, 7 A B E, 8 A, 9 B, 10 C, 11 B, 12 B, 13 A, 14 8, 15 D, 16 B C D, 17 C, 18 A, 19 D, 20 B, 21 D, 22 B, 23 20, 24 C D, 25 A B D

**1. B.** 2n + 3 = 19, so 2n = 16 and n = 8.

**2. B.** Speed = 150/2.5 = 60 miles per hour, which is less than 65.

**3. D.** 25% of 120 is 30. Sale price = 120 - 30 = 90. Choice A is the discount, not the price.

**4. 10.** Son = s, Sara = 4s. In 20 years: 4s + 20 = 2(s + 20), so 2s = 20 and s = 10. Check: 40 and 10 now, 60 and 30 later.

**5. C.** Rates: 1/6 + 1/3 = 1/2 pool per hour. Time = 2 hours. Equal.

**6. E.** Total points = 20 * 80 + 30 * 90 = 4,300. Average = 4,300/50 = 86. Choice D (85) is the unweighted average.

**7. A, B, E.** 90 km/h = 90,000 m per 3,600 s = 25 m/s. Per minute: 90/60 = 1.5 km = 1,500 m, so (B) is right and (C) is 10 times too small. Per hour it is 90,000 m, so (D) is wrong. Per day: 90 * 24 = 2,160 km.

**8. A.** Profit = 10. As a percent of cost: 10/40 = 25%. As a percent of selling price: 10/50 = 20%.

**9. B.** Tea or coffee = 30 + 25 - 10 = 45. Neither = 50 - 45 = 5.

**10. C.** n + (n + 2) + (n + 4) = 78, so 3n = 72 and n = 24. The integers are 24, 26, 28. The greatest is 28.

**11. B.** Time out = 120/40 = 3 hours. Time back = 120/60 = 2 hours. Average speed = 240/5 = 48. Choice C (50) is the plain average of the speeds.

**12. B.** Acid = 0.3 * 10 + 0.5 * 15 = 3 + 7.5 = 10.5 litres out of 25. That is 42%, less than 45%.

**13. A.** Closing speed = 80 + 60 = 140 km/h. Time = 420/140 = 3 hours.

**14. 8.** Net rate = 1/12 + 1/6 - 1/8 = 2/24 + 4/24 - 3/24 = 3/24 = 1/8 tank per hour. Time = 8 hours.

**15. D.** The overall percent is a weighted average of 60% and 40%. With equal numbers of men and women it is exactly 50%. With more men it is above 50%, and with more women it is below.

**16. B, C, D.** Both is at least 25 + 22 - 40 = 7 (when every student plays something) and at most 22 (the smaller group). Every whole number from 7 to 22 is possible. So 7, 12 and 22.

**17. C.** By 10:30 Omar is 50 * 1.5 = 75 km ahead. The gap closes at 75 - 50 = 25 km/h. Time = 75/25 = 3 hours after 10:30, which is 1:30 p.m. Check: Omar 4.5 h * 50 = 225 km, Rana 3 h * 75 = 225 km.

**18. A.** Salt = 0.4 * 20 = 8 litres, and it does not change. 8/(20 + w) = 0.25, so 20 + w = 32 and w = 12. That is greater than 10.

**19. D.** B's rate = 1/4 - 1/6 = 3/12 - 2/12 = 1/12 job per day. B alone takes 12 days. Choice C (10) adds the times and choice A (2.4) treats 4 and 6 as two workers' solo times.

**20. B.** At least one subject = 50 + 40 + 35 - 15 - 12 - 10 + 5 = 93. None = 100 - 93 = 7.

**21. D.** 24/(10 - c) + 24/(10 + c) = 5. Back-solve: c = 2 gives 24/8 + 24/12 = 3 + 2 = 5. Algebra: 480/(100 - c^2) = 5, so c^2 = 4.

**22. B.** Let each half be d miles. Total time = d/40 + d/v, which is more than d/40. So average speed = 2d/(d/40 + d/v) < 2d/(d/40) = 80. However fast the second half is, the average stays below twice the first-half speed.

**23. 20.** Together they do 1/12 of the job per hour, so in 4 hours they finish 1/3. B does the remaining 2/3 in 20 hours, so B's rate is (2/3)/20 = 1/30. A's rate = 1/12 - 1/30 = 5/60 - 2/60 = 1/20. A alone takes 20 hours.

**24. C, D.** Acid fraction = (0.2x + 5)/(x + 10). It equals 40% when 0.2x + 5 = 0.4x + 4, so x = 5. It equals 30% when 0.2x + 5 = 0.3x + 3, so x = 20. More of the weak solution lowers the percent, so 5 < x < 20. Only 8 and 15 qualify. 5 and 20 give exactly 40% and 30%, which are excluded.

**25. A, B, D.** Let the cost be 100 and the price 120. (A) Price 132: profit 32/100 = 32%, yes. (B) Cost 90: profit 30/90 = 33.3%, yes. (C) Price 129.6: profit 29.6%, no. (D) Cost 95, price 123.6: profit 28.6/95 = 30.1%, yes. (E) Price 144, cost 112: profit 32/112 = 28.6%, no.
