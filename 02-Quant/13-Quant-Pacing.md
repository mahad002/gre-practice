# Quant pacing and calculator strategy

Knowing the math is necessary; converting it into points under 21- and 26-minute limits is a separate skill.

## 1. Section budgets

| Section | Questions | Time | Useful checkpoints |
|---|---:|---:|---|
| Quant 1 | 12 | 21 min | After Q4: about 14 min left · after Q8: about 7 min left |
| Quant 2 | 15 | 26 min | After Q5: about 17 min left · after Q10: about 8–9 min left |

These are warning lights, not quotas. Data Interpretation sets may take longer at first and then become faster because the same display serves several questions.

## 2. The two-pass rule

**Pass 1:** take every clean point.

- If you do not see a viable setup after about 45 seconds, mark and move.
- If the algebra or arithmetic is expanding without a clear end after about 2 minutes, estimate/guess, mark and move.
- Finish every question you can solve reliably before donating time to one difficult item.

**Pass 2:** return in this order:

1. questions with a known method but unfinished calculation;
2. questions where two answer choices remain;
3. questions requiring a new insight.

Never leave an answer blank. A strategic guess is not failure; spending four minutes and then guessing is.

## 3. Four-line process

For every problem, make the process visible:

1. **Asked:** circle or write the exact target and unit.
2. **Given:** translate only the facts needed.
3. **Method:** choose algebra, plug-in, backsolve, estimate or test cases.
4. **Check:** sign, size, unit and whether the question asks for x, 2x, a percent or a difference.

This prevents many “careless” errors that are actually process errors.

## 4. Calculator rules

Use the on-screen calculator for tedious arithmetic, roots and multi-digit computation. Do not use it automatically.

- Estimate first so a key-entry error looks wrong.
- Keep fractions exact when the answer requires a fraction or comparison.
- Prefer mental arithmetic for simple products, powers and percent benchmarks.
- In Data Interpretation, write the requested ratio or percent before entering numbers.
- Practise with the POWERPREP calculator; a phone calculator is not a faithful substitute.

## 5. Quantitative Comparison

- Simplify both quantities before calculating.
- Test legal edge cases: 0, 1, negatives, fractions, equal values and extremes.
- Choose D only after producing two valid cases with different relationships.
- Never infer scale, equality, perpendicularity or parallelism from an unmarked geometry figure.

## 6. Timing diagnosis

After each timed section, label every over-budget question:

- **Slow setup:** you did not recognize the type.
- **Slow execution:** arithmetic/algebra took too long.
- **Bad persistence:** you should have skipped earlier.
- **Review failure:** time remained but was not used on flagged items.

Each label has a different fix. “Do more timed questions” is not a complete diagnosis.
