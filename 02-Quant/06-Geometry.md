# Geometry

Geometry is one of the four official Quant content areas, but ETS does not promise a fixed number of geometry questions on a given test. The facts are few and stable, so memorising a short list pays off fast. Hard questions combine facts (for example a 30-60-90 triangle inside a circle) or hide a “cannot be determined” result behind a figure that looks obvious.

Study plan: about 2 study days. Day 1: sections 1 to 3 and practice 1 to 10. Day 2: practice 11 to 25, then review every miss and redo it without notes.

## 1. What you must know

**Lines and angles**

1. A straight line is 180 degrees. Angles around a point add to 360. Example: angles on a line are x and 2x, so 3x = 180 and x = 60.
2. Vertical angles (opposite each other where two lines cross) are equal. Example: if one angle at a crossing is 40, the opposite one is 40 and the other two are 140.
3. Parallel lines cut by a transversal: only two angle sizes appear. All "big" angles are equal, all "small" angles are equal, and big + small = 180. Example: if one angle is 110, every angle at both crossings is 110 or 70.
4. Named pairs for parallel lines: corresponding angles are equal, alternate interior angles are equal, same-side interior angles add to 180.

**Triangles**

5. Angle sum is 180. Example: angles 50 and 60 give a third angle of 70.
6. Exterior angle = sum of the two remote interior angles. Example: interior angles 50 and 60, so the exterior angle at the third vertex is 110.
7. Bigger angle faces the longer side. Equal angles face equal sides. Example: angles 59, 60, 61: the side opposite 61 is the longest.
8. Isosceles: two equal sides and two equal base angles. Equilateral: all sides equal, all angles 60.
9. Triangle inequality: each side is less than the sum and greater than the difference of the other two. Example: sides 7 and 12, so 5 < third side < 19.
10. Area = (1/2) * base * height. The height must be perpendicular to the base. Example: base 10, height 6, area 30.
11. Pythagoras (right triangles only): a^2 + b^2 = c^2, with c the hypotenuse. Example: legs 6 and 8 give hypotenuse 10.
12. Triples to memorise: 3-4-5, 5-12-13, 8-15-17, 7-24-25, and their multiples (6-8-10, 9-12-15, 10-24-26).
13. 45-45-90 triangle: sides x, x, x * sqrt(2). Example: legs 5, hypotenuse 5 * sqrt(2). This is half of a square.
14. 30-60-90 triangle: sides x, x * sqrt(3), 2x (opposite 30, 60, 90). Example: hypotenuse 12 gives short leg 6 and long leg 6 * sqrt(3). This is half of an equilateral triangle.
15. Equilateral triangle with side s: height = s * sqrt(3) / 2, area = s^2 * sqrt(3) / 4. Example: s = 4, area = 4 * sqrt(3).
16. Similar triangles have equal angles and proportional sides. If the side ratio is k, perimeters have ratio k and areas have ratio k^2. Example: sides doubled means area times 4.
17. A line parallel to one side of a triangle cuts off a smaller similar triangle. Example: if it cuts the other two sides at their midpoints, the small triangle has half the side lengths and 1/4 of the area.
18. Obtuse or acute test, with c the longest side: c^2 > a^2 + b^2 means obtuse, c^2 < a^2 + b^2 means acute, equal means right. Example: 4, 5, 7: 49 > 41, obtuse.
19. For two fixed sides a and b, the area is largest when they are perpendicular: maximum area = (1/2) * a * b. The area can be any positive number up to that maximum.

**Quadrilaterals**

20. Angle sum is 360.
21. Rectangle: area = length * width, perimeter = 2 * (length + width), diagonal = sqrt(l^2 + w^2). Diagonals are equal.
22. Square with side s: area s^2, diagonal s * sqrt(2), so area = diagonal^2 / 2. Example: diagonal 10 gives area 50.
23. Parallelogram: area = base * height (not base * slanted side). Opposite sides and opposite angles are equal. Neighbouring angles add to 180.
24. Rhombus: four equal sides. Diagonals cross at right angles and cut each other in half. Area = (d1 * d2) / 2. Example: diagonals 6 and 8 give area 24 and side 5.
25. Trapezoid: area = (1/2) * (b1 + b2) * height, where b1 and b2 are the parallel sides. Example: parallel sides 8 and 20, height 8, area 112.

**Polygons**

26. Sum of interior angles of an n-sided polygon = (n - 2) * 180. Example: hexagon, 4 * 180 = 720.
27. Regular polygon: each interior angle = (n - 2) * 180 / n, each exterior angle = 360 / n. Example: regular hexagon, interior 120, exterior 60.
28. A regular hexagon with side s is six equilateral triangles: area = 6 * s^2 * sqrt(3) / 4.

**Circles**

29. Circumference = 2 * pi * r = pi * d. Area = pi * r^2. Example: r = 5, circumference 10 * pi, area 25 * pi. Use pi = 3.14 (a little more than 3) for comparisons.
30. Arc length = (angle / 360) * 2 * pi * r. Sector area = (angle / 360) * pi * r^2. The fraction of the circle is the same for the angle, the arc and the sector. Example: 60 degrees is 1/6 of everything.
31. A chord joins two points on the circle. The diameter is the longest chord. A perpendicular from the centre to a chord cuts the chord in half. Example: radius 13, chord 10, so the distance from the centre to the chord is sqrt(169 - 25) = 12.
32. A tangent touches the circle at one point and is perpendicular to the radius at that point. Two tangents from the same outside point are equal in length.
33. Central angle = 2 * inscribed angle on the same arc. Example: inscribed angle 35, central angle 70.
34. An angle inscribed in a semicircle is 90. So a triangle with a diameter as one side and the third vertex on the circle is a right triangle.
35. Square inscribed in a circle: the diagonal of the square = the diameter. Circle inscribed in a square: the side of the square = the diameter.
36. Radius of the circle inscribed in a right triangle with legs a, b and hypotenuse c: r = (a + b - c) / 2. For any triangle: r = area / (half the perimeter).

**3D solids**

37. Rectangular box l by w by h: volume = l * w * h, surface area = 2 * (lw + lh + wh), space diagonal = sqrt(l^2 + w^2 + h^2). Example: 3 by 4 by 12 has diagonal 13.
38. Cube with edge s: volume s^3, surface area 6 * s^2, space diagonal s * sqrt(3).
39. Cylinder with radius r and height h: volume = pi * r^2 * h, surface area = 2 * pi * r^2 + 2 * pi * r * h (two circles plus the side).
40. Displacement: an object fully under water raises the water by (volume of object) / (area of the tank's base).

**GRE figure rules and max/min ideas**

41. Geometric figures are NOT drawn to scale. Do not trust how long a side looks or how big an angle looks. You may trust: straight lines are straight, points lie on lines in the order shown, and shapes are what they are labelled. Nothing is a right angle, parallel, or equal unless stated or marked. (Coordinate grids and data graphs ARE drawn to scale.)
42. For a fixed perimeter, the square has the largest area of all rectangles. For a fixed area, the square has the smallest perimeter. Example: perimeter 20 gives at most area 25.
43. A rectangle with a fixed perimeter can have an area as close to 0 as you like (very long and thin). So "area of a rectangle with perimeter 20" is any value from just above 0 up to 25.
44. In Quantitative Comparison, if a shape can be stretched or squashed while still fitting the given facts, try the extreme versions. If the comparison changes, the answer is D.

## 2. Worked examples

**Example 1 (Quantitative Comparison)**

In triangle ABC, AB = 5 and BC = 8.

Quantity A: The length of AC
Quantity B: 13

Solution:
1. Triangle inequality: AC < AB + BC = 13.
2. AC is always less than 13, whatever the shape.
3. Answer: B.

The trap: choosing D because "the triangle is not fixed". The triangle is not fixed, but the comparison is. D is only correct when the comparison itself can change.

**Example 2 (Multiple choice, one answer)**

In triangle ABC, angle C = 90 degrees, angle A = 30 degrees and AB = 12. What is the area of the triangle?

(A) 18  (B) 18 * sqrt(3)  (C) 36  (D) 36 * sqrt(3)  (E) 72

Solution:
1. This is a 30-60-90 triangle. The hypotenuse AB = 2x = 12, so x = 6.
2. BC is opposite the 30 degree angle: BC = 6. AC is opposite 60: AC = 6 * sqrt(3).
3. Area = (1/2) * 6 * 6 * sqrt(3) = 18 * sqrt(3).
4. Answer: B.

The trap: using the hypotenuse as a base or height, which gives 36 or 72.

**Example 3 (Multiple choice, one answer)**

A circle has centre O and radius 6. Points A and B are on the circle and angle AOB = 60 degrees. What is the area of the region between chord AB and the minor arc AB?

(A) 6 * pi  (B) 6 * pi - 18  (C) 12 * pi - 9 * sqrt(3)  (D) 6 * pi - 9 * sqrt(3)  (E) 36 * pi - 9 * sqrt(3)

Solution:
1. Region = sector AOB minus triangle AOB.
2. Sector = (60 / 360) * pi * 36 = 6 * pi.
3. Triangle AOB has OA = OB = 6 and a 60 degree angle between them, so it is equilateral with side 6. Area = 36 * sqrt(3) / 4 = 9 * sqrt(3).
4. Region = 6 * pi - 9 * sqrt(3). Answer: D.

Fastest check: 6 * pi - 9 * sqrt(3) is about 18.85 - 15.59 = 3.3, a thin sliver. That fits the picture.

The trap: stopping at the sector (A).

**Example 4 (Numeric entry)**

In triangle ABC, point D is on side AB and point E is on side AC. DE is parallel to BC. AD = 4, DB = 6 and DE = 6. What is the length of BC?

```
        A
       / \
      D---E      DE is parallel to BC
     /     \
    B-------C
```

Solution:
1. DE parallel to BC makes triangle ADE similar to triangle ABC.
2. The matching sides are AD and AB (not AD and DB). AB = 4 + 6 = 10.
3. DE / BC = AD / AB, so 6 / BC = 4 / 10 and BC = 15.
4. Answer: 15.

The trap: using the ratio 4 : 6, which gives BC = 9. The ratio must compare whole sides of the two triangles.

**Example 5 (Quantitative Comparison)**

A rectangle has perimeter 20.

Quantity A: The area of the rectangle
Quantity B: 25

Solution:
1. Length + width = 10.
2. If the rectangle is a 5 by 5 square, area = 25. The quantities are equal.
3. If it is 9 by 1, area = 9. Quantity B is greater.
4. Two different outcomes. Answer: D.

The trap: forgetting that a square is a rectangle and choosing B.

**Example 6 (Select all that apply)**

Two sides of a triangle have lengths 6 and 10. Which of the following could be the area of the triangle? Select all that apply.

(A) 5  (B) 18  (C) 30  (D) 31  (E) 45

Solution:
1. Take the side of length 10 as the base. The height is at most 6, reached when the two sides are perpendicular.
2. Maximum area = (1/2) * 10 * 6 = 30. By closing the angle, the area can be any positive value below 30.
3. So the area can be 5, 18 or 30, but not 31 or 45.
4. Answer: A, B, C.

The trap: thinking the area must be exactly 30, or that the third side is needed.

**Example 7 (Multiple choice, one answer)**

Points A, B and C lie on a circle with centre O and radius 9. AC is a diameter and angle BAC = 35 degrees. What is the length of minor arc BC?

(A) 7 * pi / 4  (B) 5 * pi / 2  (C) 7 * pi / 2  (D) 7 * pi  (E) 35 * pi / 2

Solution:
1. Angle BAC is an inscribed angle that stands on arc BC.
2. The central angle on the same arc is double: angle BOC = 70 degrees.
3. Arc BC = (70 / 360) * 2 * pi * 9 = (7 / 36) * 18 * pi = 7 * pi / 2.
4. Answer: C.

The trap: using 35 degrees in the arc formula, which gives (A). Arc length always uses the central angle.

**Example 8 (Multiple choice, one answer)**

A right circular cylinder has radius 3 and height 8. What is the greatest possible distance between two points on the cylinder?

(A) 8  (B) sqrt(73)  (C) 10  (D) 11  (E) 14

Solution:
1. The two farthest points are on opposite edges: one on the rim of the top circle, the other on the rim of the bottom circle, at opposite ends of a diameter.
2. Slice the cylinder through its axis. The slice is a rectangle with width = diameter = 6 and height 8.
3. The distance is the diagonal of this rectangle: sqrt(36 + 64) = 10.
4. Answer: C.

The trap: using the radius instead of the diameter, which gives sqrt(73).

## 3. Common traps

- Trusting the drawing. A figure that looks like a right triangle or a square is not one unless the question says so.
- Using a slanted side as the height of a triangle, parallelogram or trapezoid. The height is always perpendicular to the base.
- Applying Pythagoras or the 3-4-5 pattern to a triangle that is not stated to be right.
- Mixing up the 30-60-90 sides. The side opposite 60 is x * sqrt(3) (about 1.73x). The hypotenuse is 2x, the largest.
- Similar figures: lengths scale by k but areas scale by k^2 and volumes by k^3. Doubling the radius gives 4 times the area.
- Radius versus diameter. Read which one is given before using any circle formula.
- Triangle inequality is strict. With sides 7 and 12, the third side cannot be exactly 5 or 19.
- Isosceles with "one angle is 50": the 50 could be the top angle (50, 65, 65) or a base angle (50, 50, 80).
- Forgetting that a square is a rectangle and a rectangle is a parallelogram. Special cases are allowed unless ruled out.
- In "greatest area" questions, the best rectangle is the square and the best angle between two fixed sides is 90 degrees.

## 5. Answers and solutions

Answer key: 1 C, 2 E, 3 D, 4 B, 5 12, 6 B, 7 D, 8 C D E, 9 A, 10 A, 11 B, 12 A, 13 D, 14 36, 15 A B D F, 16 D, 17 A B C D, 18 C, 19 A, 20 B, 21 B, 22 A B E F, 23 3, 24 C, 25 D

**1. C.** Same-side interior angles between parallel lines add to 180. (3x + 10) + (2x + 20) = 180, so 5x = 150 and x = 30. Equal.

**2. E.** 2k + 3k + 4k = 180, so k = 20. The largest angle is 4k = 80.

**3. D.** 9-12-15 is 3 times the 3-4-5 triple, so the hypotenuse is 15. Perimeter = 9 + 12 + 15 = 36.

**4. B.** 2 * pi * r = 10 * pi gives r = 5. Area = 25 * pi, about 78.5. That is less than 80.

**5. 12.** Each exterior angle = 180 - 150 = 30. Number of sides = 360 / 30 = 12.

**6. B.** Width w, length 2w. w^2 + 4w^2 = (5 * sqrt(5))^2 = 125, so w^2 = 25 and w = 5. Area = 5 * 10 = 50.

**7. D.** If 50 is a base angle, the angles are 50, 50, 80 and the largest is 80 (equal). If 50 is the top angle, the angles are 50, 65, 65 and the largest is 65 (B greater). Two outcomes.

**8. C, D, E.** 12 - 7 < third side < 12 + 7, so 5 < third side < 19. The values 6, 12 and 18 work. 5 and 19 fail because the inequality is strict.

**9. A.** Edge = cube root of 64 = 4. Surface area = 6 * 16 = 96.

**10. A.** Side = 10 / sqrt(2) = 5 * sqrt(2), about 7.07. Perimeter = 20 * sqrt(2), about 28.28, which is more than 28. Calculator-free check: 28.28^2 = 800 and 28^2 = 784.

**11. B.** Circumference = 24 * pi. The arc is 4 * pi / (24 * pi) = 1/6 of the circle. Sector = (1/6) * pi * 144 = 24 * pi.

**12. A.** Angle R = 180 - 59 - 61 = 60. Side PQ is opposite angle R (60). Side QR is opposite angle P (59). The bigger angle faces the longer side, so PQ > QR.

**13. D.** Drop perpendiculars from A and B to DC. The leftover pieces of DC on each side are (20 - 8) / 2 = 6. Each forms a right triangle with hypotenuse 10 and leg 6, so the height is 8. Area = (1/2) * (8 + 20) * 8 = 112.

**14. 36.** s^2 * sqrt(3) / 4 = 36 * sqrt(3) gives s^2 = 144 and s = 12. Perimeter = 36.

**15. A, B, D, F.** List the integer boxes with volume 36 and find 2 * (lw + lh + wh): 1 by 1 by 36 gives 146. 1 by 2 by 18 gives 112. 1 by 3 by 12 gives 102. 1 by 4 by 9 gives 98. 1 by 6 by 6 gives 96. 2 by 2 by 9 gives 80. 2 by 3 by 6 gives 72. 3 by 3 by 4 gives 66. So 66, 72, 96 and 146 are possible. 84 and 100 are not.

**16. D.** The longest chord is the diameter, so 10 <= 2r and r >= 5. If the chord is a diameter, r = 5 (equal). Otherwise r > 5 (A greater). Two outcomes.

**17. A, B, C, D.** Take AB = 10 as the base. The height is the distance from C to AB, which is at most the radius, 5. So the area is at most (1/2) * 10 * 5 = 25, and it can be any positive value up to 25. 26 and 30 are too big.

**18. C.** The diagonals of a rhombus cross at right angles and bisect each other. Half of the known diagonal is 3. With side 5, half of the other diagonal is sqrt(25 - 9) = 4, so the other diagonal is 8. Area = 6 * 8 / 2 = 24. Equal.

**19. A.** AB = 10 (6-8-10). Compute the area two ways: (1/2) * 6 * 8 = 24 and (1/2) * 10 * CD = 24. So CD = 4.8.

**20. B.** Volume of the cube = 216. Base area of the tank = pi * 36. Rise = 216 / (36 * pi) = 6 / pi.

**21. B.** The diagonal of the rectangle is a diameter, 10. With sides a and b: a^2 + b^2 = 100. The perimeter is largest for the square: a = b = 5 * sqrt(2), perimeter = 20 * sqrt(2), about 28.3. Proof: (a + b)^2 = 100 + 2ab, and 2ab <= a^2 + b^2 = 100, so a + b <= sqrt(200). Every such rectangle has perimeter at most 28.3, which is less than 29.

**22. A, B, E, F.** First, 7 < x < 23 for a triangle. If x is the longest side: obtuse when x^2 > 64 + 225 = 289, so x > 17. That gives 18 and 22. If 15 is the longest side: obtuse when 225 > 64 + x^2, so x^2 < 161 and x <= 12. That gives 9 and 12. Check the others: x = 13 gives 64 + 169 = 233 > 225, acute. x = 17 gives a right triangle (8-15-17).

**23. 3.** Hypotenuse = 17 (8-15-17). r = (a + b - c) / 2 = (8 + 15 - 17) / 2 = 3. Check with r = area / half-perimeter = 60 / 20 = 3.

**24. C.** Radius OA is perpendicular to tangent PA, so triangle OAP is right with OA = 5, OP = 13 and PA = 12. Chord AB is perpendicular to OP, and half of AB is the height of triangle OAP from A to OP. Area two ways: (1/2) * 5 * 12 = (1/2) * 13 * h, so h = 60 / 13. AB = 2h = 120 / 13. The trap is (A), which is only half the chord.

**25. D.** Let the centres be P and Q and let the circles cross at X and Y. PQ = PX = QX = 6, so triangle PQX is equilateral and angle XPY = 120 degrees. In each circle, the overlap contains a segment cut off by chord XY: sector (120 / 360) * pi * 36 = 12 * pi minus triangle PXY = (1/2) * 6 * 6 * sin(120) = 9 * sqrt(3). The overlap is two such segments: 2 * (12 * pi - 9 * sqrt(3)) = 24 * pi - 18 * sqrt(3), about 44.2. If you do not know sin(120): triangle PXY has base XY = 6 * sqrt(3) and height 3, area 9 * sqrt(3).
