# Fractions, Percents and Ratios

Percents and ratios appear in a large share of GRE Quant questions, including most Data Interpretation sets and many word problems. The maths is easy. The marks are lost on wording: "percent of" against "percent greater than", and "of what base".
Study plan (about 2 days): day 1 = read sections 1 to 3 and do practice 1 to 10. Day 2 = practice 11 to 25, then review every problem you missed or guessed.

## 1. What you must know

**Fraction operations**

1. Add or subtract: use a common denominator. Example: 3/8 + 5/12 = 9/24 + 10/24 = 19/24.
2. Multiply: top times top, bottom times bottom. Cancel first. Example: (4/9)(3/8) = 12/72 = 1/6.
3. Divide: multiply by the reciprocal. Example: (2/3) / (4/5) = (2/3)(5/4) = 5/6.
4. "Of" means multiply. Example: 2/5 of 60 = 24.
5. Mixed numbers: convert to improper fractions before multiplying or dividing. Example: 2 1/3 = 7/3.
6. You can never split a denominator. (a + b)/c = a/c + b/c is true, but a/(b + c) is not a/b + a/c. Example: 6/(1 + 2) = 2, not 6/1 + 6/2 = 9.

**Comparing fractions**

7. Cross-multiply (for positive fractions): compare a/b and c/d by comparing a*d and b*c. Example: 7/11 vs 9/14: 7*14 = 98 and 9*11 = 99, so 9/14 is greater.
8. Same numerator: the smaller denominator gives the larger fraction. Example: 5/7 > 5/9.
9. Adding the same positive number to top and bottom moves a positive fraction toward 1. Example: 2/3 < 3/4 < 4/5, and 5/4 > 6/5.
10. Benchmarks: compare each fraction with 1/2 or 1. Example: 5/11 < 1/2 < 7/13.
11. On the real test, the calculator settles any numeric comparison. Use it when the numbers are ugly.

**Conversions to memorise**

12. Fraction to percent table:
    - 1/2 = 0.5 = 50%
    - 1/3 = 0.333... = 33 1/3%, 2/3 = 0.666... = 66 2/3%
    - 1/4 = 0.25 = 25%, 3/4 = 0.75 = 75%
    - 1/5 = 0.2 = 20%, 2/5 = 40%, 3/5 = 60%, 4/5 = 80%
    - 1/6 = 0.1666... = 16 2/3%, 5/6 = 0.8333... = 83 1/3%
    - 1/8 = 0.125 = 12.5%, 3/8 = 37.5%, 5/8 = 62.5%, 7/8 = 87.5%
    - 1/9 = 0.111... = 11.1%, 1/10 = 10%, 1/11 = 0.0909... = 9.09%, 1/12 = 0.0833... = 8 1/3%
    - 1/20 = 5%, 1/25 = 4%, 1/50 = 2%, 1/100 = 1%
13. Percent to decimal: move the point two places left. Example: 7.5% = 0.075, 250% = 2.5, 0.4% = 0.004.

**Percent basics**

14. Percent of: part = (percent/100) * whole. Example: 35% of 80 = 0.35 * 80 = 28.
15. x% of y equals y% of x. Example: 16% of 25 = 25% of 16 = 4.
16. "What percent of B is A?" means A/B * 100. The number after "of" is the base. Example: 12 is what percent of 48? 12/48 = 25%.
17. Percent change = (new - old)/old * 100. The base is always the old (original) value. Example: 40 to 52 is 12/40 = 30% increase.
18. Multipliers: an increase of p% multiplies by (1 + p/100). A decrease multiplies by (1 - p/100). Example: up 30% is times 1.3, down 15% is times 0.85.
19. "Percent of" vs "percent greater than": 150% of 40 is 60. 150% greater than 40 is 40 * 2.5 = 100. "200% of" means double, "200% more" means triple.
20. Reversing a change: if A is p% more than B, B is not p% less than A. Use multipliers. Example: A = 1.25B means B = A/1.25 = 0.8A, so B is 20% less than A.
21. To find the original before a change, divide by the multiplier. Example: a price after a 20% discount is 60, so the original is 60/0.8 = 75, not 72.
22. Percent points are not percents. A rate going from 10% to 12% rises by 2 percentage points, which is a 20% increase.

**Successive percent changes**

23. Multiply the multipliers. Never add the percents. Example: up 20% then down 20% is 1.2 * 0.8 = 0.96, a 4% decrease.
24. Up p% then down p% always gives a net decrease of p^2/100 percent. Example: p = 30 gives a 9% decrease.
25. The order of successive changes does not matter. Example: 20% off then 10% off equals 10% off then 20% off, which is 0.72 of the original (28% off, not 30%).
26. When no starting value is given, use 100.

**Interest**

27. Simple interest: interest = P * r * t, where r is the annual rate as a decimal and t is in years. Total = P(1 + rt). Example: 2,000 at 5% for 3 years earns 300.
28. Compound interest: total = P(1 + r/n)^(n*t), where n is the number of compounding periods per year. Example: 5,000 at 8% compounded semiannually for 1 year = 5,000 * 1.04^2 = 5,408.
29. Compound interest is always more than simple interest at the same rate once there is more than one period. Example: 10% for 2 years: simple gives 20%, compound gives 21%.

**Ratios and proportions**

30. A ratio a:b compares parts. The total is a + b parts. Example: boys to girls 3:5 means boys are 3/8 of the total, not 3/5.
31. Use a multiplier: write the real amounts as 3k and 5k. Example: 3k + 5k = 40 gives k = 5, so 15 boys and 25 girls.
32. If the things counted are whole objects, the total must be a multiple of the sum of the ratio parts. Example: ratio 3:8 means the total is a multiple of 11.
33. A ratio alone tells you nothing about actual amounts. This is a classic QC answer of D.
34. Proportion: a/b = c/d means ad = bc. Example: 3/4 = x/20 gives x = 15.
35. Combining ratios: make the shared term equal. Example: a:b = 2:3 and b:c = 4:5. Scale b to 12: a:b = 8:12, b:c = 12:15, so a:b:c = 8:12:15.
36. Adding the same amount to both parts changes a ratio. Multiplying both parts by the same amount does not. Example: 2:3 becomes 3:4 if you add 1 to each.

**Direct and inverse proportion**

37. Direct: y = kx. The ratio y/x stays constant. Double x and y doubles. Example: 5 pens cost 8 dollars, so 15 pens cost 24 dollars.
38. Inverse: y = k/x. The product x*y stays constant. Double x and y halves. Example: 8 workers take 15 days, so 12 workers take 8*15/12 = 10 days.
39. Variations: "y varies inversely as the square of x" means y = k/x^2. Find k from the given pair, then substitute. Example: y = 9 at x = 2 gives k = 36, so y = 1 at x = 6.

## 2. Worked examples

**Example 1 (Quantitative Comparison)**
Quantity A: 13/17
Quantity B: 16/21

Solution: Cross-multiply. 13 * 21 = 273 and 16 * 17 = 272. Since 273 > 272, 13/17 is greater. Calculator check: 0.7647 vs 0.7619.
The trap: both fractions are "4 and 5 short of 1" so they look almost equal. Do not guess C on a look.
Answer: A.

**Example 2 (Multiple choice, one answer)**
The price of a shirt fell from $80 to $68. By what percent did the price decrease?
(A) 12% (B) 15% (C) 17.6% (D) 18% (E) 85%

Solution: Change = 12. Base = old price = 80. 12/80 = 0.15 = 15%.
The trap: 12/68 = 17.6% uses the new price as the base. 12% is the dollar change, not the percent. 85% is "percent of", not the decrease.
Answer: B.

**Example 3 (Multiple choice, one answer)**
A price is increased by 30% and the new price is then decreased by 30%. The final price is what percent of the original price?
(A) 70% (B) 91% (C) 97% (D) 100% (E) 109%

Solution: Start at 100. Up 30% gives 130. Down 30% of 130 is 39, leaving 91. Or 1.3 * 0.7 = 0.91.
The trap: 100%, because +30 and -30 "cancel". They do not, because the second 30% is taken from a bigger base.
Answer: B.

**Example 4 (Numeric entry)**
x and y are positive, and x is 25% greater than y. y is what percent less than x? Give the answer as a number of percent.

Solution: x = 1.25y. Plug in y = 100, x = 125. y is less than x by 25, and the base is now x: 25/125 = 20%.
The trap: answering 25. The base changed from y to x.
Answer: 20.

**Example 5 (Multiple choice, one answer)**
$4,000 is invested at 10% annual interest, compounded annually. How much interest is earned in 3 years?
(A) $1,200 (B) $1,240 (C) $1,324 (D) $1,331 (E) $5,324

Solution: Total = 4,000 * 1.1^3 = 4,000 * 1.331 = 5,324. Interest = 5,324 - 4,000 = 1,324.
The trap: $1,200 is simple interest. $5,324 is the total, not the interest. Always check which one is asked.
Answer: C.

**Example 6 (Select all that apply)**
The ratio of cats to dogs at a shelter is 3 to 8, and there are only cats and dogs. Which of the following could be the total number of animals?
(A) 22 (B) 30 (C) 44 (D) 64 (E) 121

Solution: Cats = 3k, dogs = 8k, total = 11k. The total must be a multiple of 11: 22, 44 and 121 work. 30 and 64 do not.
The trap: looking for multiples of 3 or 8 instead of 3 + 8.
Answer: A, C and E.

**Example 7 (Quantitative Comparison)**
a, b and c are positive. The ratio of a to b is 2 to 5, and the ratio of b to c is 3 to 4.
Quantity A: a/c
Quantity B: 1/3

Solution: Make b the same in both ratios. LCM of 5 and 3 is 15. a:b = 6:15 and b:c = 15:20. So a:c = 6:20 and a/c = 3/10 = 0.3. That is less than 1/3.
Fastest method: a/c = (a/b)(b/c) = (2/5)(3/4) = 6/20.
The trap: reading a:c straight off as 2:4.
Answer: B.

**Example 8 (Multiple choice, one answer)**
Working at the same constant rate, 8 workers can paint a building in 15 days. How many days would 12 workers, working at that rate, take?
(A) 8 (B) 10 (C) 11 (D) 19 (E) 22.5

Solution: More workers means fewer days, so this is inverse proportion. Total work = 8 * 15 = 120 worker-days. 120/12 = 10 days.
The trap: setting up a direct proportion, 8/15 = 12/x, which gives 22.5. Ask "should the answer go up or down?" before calculating.
Answer: B.

## 3. Common traps

- Wrong base. Percent change always divides by the original value. "A is what percent greater than B" divides by B.
- Adding successive percents. +20% then +30% is 1.2 * 1.3 = 1.56, a 56% increase, not 50%.
- Thinking +p% and -p% cancel. They always leave you below the start.
- "Percent of" vs "percent more than". 120% of x is 1.2x. 120% more than x is 2.2x.
- Part-to-part vs part-to-whole. A ratio of 3:5 means 3/8 and 5/8 of the total.
- Assuming real amounts from a ratio or a percent. Without one actual number, amounts are unknown (often answer D in QC).
- Averaging percents from groups of different sizes. You need a weighted average.
- Finding the original price by taking the percent of the new price. Divide by the multiplier instead.
- Direct vs inverse proportion. Check whether the answer should rise or fall.
- Compounding periods. "8% annual, compounded quarterly" means 2% per quarter, 4 times a year.

## 5. Answers and solutions

**Answer key:** 1 C, 2 C, 3 D, 4 B, 5 25, 6 B, 7 B C E, 8 B, 9 C, 10 A, 11 B, 12 A, 13 C, 14 30, 15 B C E F, 16 D, 17 B, 18 B, 19 A, 20 B C D E, 21 D, 22 A, 23 28, 24 E, 25 A D

**1. C.** Common denominator 24: 9/24 + 10/24 = 19/24. Choice A comes from adding tops and bottoms (8/20), which is never valid.

**2. C.** 0.35 * 80 = 28 and 0.80 * 35 = 28. x% of y always equals y% of x.

**3. D.** Change = 12, original = 40. 12/40 = 30%. Choice B (23%) uses 52 as the base.

**4. B.** Cross-multiply: 7 * 14 = 98 and 9 * 11 = 99. Since 98 < 99, 7/11 < 9/14. (0.6364 vs 0.6429.)

**5. 25.** 3k + 5k = 40, so k = 5. Girls = 5k = 25.

**6. B.** 15% of 400 = 60. 2/5 of 60 = 24.

**7. B, C, E.** 2/3 = 0.6667. The values are 0.625, 0.7, 0.6875, 0.65, 0.68, 0.6333. Only 7/10, 11/16 and 17/25 are above 0.6667.

**8. B.** Final = original * 1.2 * 0.8 = 0.96 * original. A price is positive, so the final price is 4% lower.

**9. C.** Interest = 2,000 * 0.05 * 3 = 300. Total = 2,300. Choice D is the compound-interest value.

**10. A.** x/z = (x/y)(y/z) = (2/3)(4/5) = 8/15 = 0.533, which is greater than 1/2. (Or x:y:z = 8:12:15.)

**11. B.** Start at 100. After Monday: 125. To get back to 100 it must fall 25 out of 125 = 20%.

**12. A.** 150% greater than 40 = 40 + 60 = 100. 150% of 60 = 90.

**13. C.** 8% per year compounded semiannually is 4% per half-year, twice. 5,000 * 1.04 * 1.04 = 5,408. Choice A is simple interest.

**14. 30.** Milk = 5k, water = 3k. After adding water: 5k/(3k + 6) = 5/4, so 3k + 6 = 4k and k = 6. Milk = 30 litres. Check: 30 to 18, then 30 to 24 = 5 to 4.

**15. B, C, E, F.** (A) fails: a = 6, b = 8 works. (B) is cross-multiplication. (C): (a + b)/b = a/b + 1 = 7/4. (D) fails: with a = 6, b = 8 it gives 7/9. (E): b = (4/3)a, which is 1/3 more than a. (F): a = (3/4)b, which is 25% less than b.

**16. D.** x = 10, y = 9: 1.10 * 0.91 = 1.001, slightly above the original. x = 50, y = 40: 1.5 * 0.6 = 0.9, below the original. A bigger percent up than down does not guarantee a net gain.

**17. B.** y = k/x^2. 9 = k/4, so k = 36. At x = 6, y = 36/36 = 1. Shortcut: x tripled, so y is divided by 9.

**18. B.** Use 100 students: 60 women and 40 men. Glasses: 0.25 * 60 = 15 women and 0.40 * 40 = 16 men. Women are 15/31 = 48.4% of the glasses wearers, which is less than 50%.

**19. A.** Let Chen = 100. Bilal = 125. Ahmed = 0.8 * 125 = 100. Ahmed's salary is 100% of Chen's.

**20. B, C, D, E.** 1.3 < 1.1(1 + r/100) < 1.4, so 1.1818 < 1 + r/100 < 1.2727, so r is between 18.18 and 27.27. That allows 19, 22, 25 and 27. Check the edges: 1.1 * 1.18 = 1.298 (too small), 1.1 * 1.28 = 1.408 (too big).

**21. D.** Make Bina the same in both ratios: A:B = 9:12 and B:C = 12:10. So A:B:C = 9:12:10. Chris minus Ali = 1 part = 18 dollars. Total = 31 parts = 558.

**22. A.** 1.1^7 = 1.949, which is less than 2. 1.1^8 = 2.144. So the least number of whole years is 8, which is greater than 7. The trap is the rule-of-thumb guess 72/10 = 7.2 rounded down to 7.

**23. 28.** New revenue = 1.25 * (1 - x/100) of the old revenue = 0.90. So 1 - x/100 = 0.90/1.25 = 0.72 and x = 28.

**24. E.** (1 + p/100)(1 - p/100) = 1 - p^2/10,000 = 0.9375. So p^2/10,000 = 0.0625, p^2 = 625, p = 25. Check: 1.25 * 0.75 = 0.9375. Back-solving from the options also works.

**25. A, D.** Last year: 3k boys and 4k girls, with k a positive integer. This year: boys = 3.6k and girls = 3.6k, total 7.2k. 3.6k must be a whole number, so k is a multiple of 5. k = 5 gives 36, k = 10 gives 72, and k = 15 gives 108, which is too many. Only 36 and 72 are possible.
