# Quiz: Week 3 (Days 11 to 15)

25 questions on the 100 words in `Words-Week-3.md`. Cover the answers at the bottom, write your answers down, then check them. Aim for 20 or more correct.

## Part A: Fill the blank

Choose the one word that best completes the sentence.

1. The guard gave the passports only a ______ glance, missing the fact that two of them had expired.

   (A) plausible  (B) cursory  (C) fervent  (D) meticulous  (E) erudite

2. Decades of ______ between the two families ended only when their children married.

   (A) conjecture  (B) consensus  (C) acumen  (D) enmity  (E) anachronism

3. The phrase was once fresh, but endless repetition in advertisements has made it ______.

   (A) novel  (B) hackneyed  (C) tenuous  (D) nascent  (E) astute

4. Only a ______ buyer would believe that a genuine diamond could cost so little.

   (A) shrewd  (B) skeptical  (C) diligent  (D) credulous  (E) judicious

5. A second laboratory ______ the finding by repeating the experiment and obtaining the same result.

   (A) refuted  (B) antagonized  (C) surmised  (D) corroborated  (E) repudiated

6. The typewriter is now ______, having been replaced almost everywhere by the computer.

   (A) affable  (B) zealous  (C) unprecedented  (D) burgeoning  (E) obsolete

7. His cheerful tone ______ the seriousness of the news he had come to deliver.

   (A) belied  (B) posited  (C) espoused  (D) concurred  (E) validated

8. An ______ worker, she stayed late every evening to check her figures a third time.

   (A) remiss  (B) apathetic  (C) assiduous  (D) obtuse  (E) indolent

9. The policy is ______: it saves a little money this year but will cost a great deal more within ten.

   (A) myopic  (B) orthodox  (C) sagacious  (D) cordial  (E) prudent

10. With no witnesses and no documents, his version of events remained pure ______.

   (A) acumen  (B) consensus  (C) conjecture  (D) discord  (E) acrimony

## Part B: Pick the two synonyms

Choose the two words that both fit the sentence and give it the same meaning.

11. It was ______ of the nurse to leave the medicine cabinet unlocked.

   (A) meticulous  (B) novel  (C) negligent  (D) conscientious  (E) remiss  (F) astute

12. The ______ editor checked every date, name and figure in the manuscript twice.

   (A) meticulous  (B) gullible  (C) cursory  (D) perfunctory  (E) bellicose  (F) fastidious

13. The general's ______ speech, full of threats against neighboring states, alarmed diplomats.

   (A) belligerent  (B) pedantic  (C) trite  (D) amicable  (E) cordial  (F) bellicose

14. The lecture was a string of ______ observations that everyone in the hall had heard many times before.

   (A) trite  (B) erudite  (C) tenuous  (D) banal  (E) unprecedented  (F) novel

15. An ______ negotiator, she saw at once what the other side wanted most and used it to her advantage.

   (A) lethargic  (B) shrewd  (C) astute  (D) gullible  (E) credulous  (F) archaic

16. Reporters were ______ about the company's claim, since it had made similar promises before and broken them.

   (A) skeptical  (B) fervent  (C) dubious  (D) zealous  (E) diligent  (F) affable

17. The divorce was settled without ______; the couple remained on friendly terms.

   (A) anomaly  (B) conjecture  (C) acumen  (D) acrimony  (E) rancor  (F) consensus

18. The ______ industry, only two years old, has not yet attracted government regulation.

   (A) incipient  (B) antiquated  (C) contentious  (D) nascent  (E) derivative  (F) obsolete

19. She has long ______ the cause of free education, speaking for it at every opportunity.

   (A) refuted  (B) repudiated  (C) espoused  (D) belied  (E) advocated  (F) surmised

20. The chatter at the party was so ______ that he slipped away early to read.

   (A) polemical  (B) plausible  (C) erudite  (D) vapid  (E) fervent  (F) inane

## Part C: Which word does not belong?

Four of the five words are close in meaning. Choose the one that is not.

21. (A) indolent  (B) industrious  (C) diligent  (D) conscientious  (E) assiduous

22. (A) acrimony  (B) consensus  (C) rancor  (D) enmity  (E) animosity

23. (A) trite  (B) banal  (C) prosaic  (D) novel  (E) hackneyed

24. (A) vindicate  (B) validate  (C) substantiate  (D) corroborate  (E) debunk

25. (A) perspicacious  (B) obtuse  (C) astute  (D) shrewd  (E) sagacious

## Answers

1. B, cursory. A glance that misses expired passports was quick and not thorough.
2. D, enmity. Something lasting decades and ended by a marriage is hostility.
3. B, hackneyed. Overuse has removed its freshness, which is the meaning of hackneyed.
4. D, credulous. Believing an unlikely claim shows too much readiness to believe.
5. D, corroborated. The same result from a repeat confirms the finding.
6. E, obsolete. Something replaced almost everywhere is no longer in use.
7. A, belied. A cheerful tone gives a false impression of serious news.
8. C, assiduous. Staying late to check a third time shows persistent hard work.
9. A, myopic. Short-term saving at long-term cost is short-sighted.
10. C, conjecture. A claim with no evidence is a guess.
11. C and E, negligent and remiss. Leaving the cabinet unlocked is a failure of proper care.
12. A and F, meticulous and fastidious. Checking everything twice shows great attention to detail. Cursory and perfunctory are opposites.
13. A and F, belligerent and bellicose. Threats against neighbors are warlike and hostile.
14. A and D, trite and banal. Remarks heard many times before are ordinary and overused.
15. B and C, shrewd and astute. Seeing and using an advantage quickly shows sharp judgment.
16. A and C, skeptical and dubious. Broken promises in the past give reason to doubt.
17. D and E, acrimony and rancor. Remaining friendly means there was no bitterness.
18. A and D, incipient and nascent. Only two years old means just beginning. Obsolete and antiquated mean out of date.
19. C and E, espoused and advocated. Speaking for a cause at every opportunity is supporting it.
20. D and F, vapid and inane. Chatter dull enough to escape from is empty and silly.
21. A, indolent. The other four describe hard, careful work. Indolent means lazy.
22. B, consensus. The other four mean hostility or bitterness. Consensus means general agreement.
23. D, novel. The other four mean ordinary or overused. Novel means new and original.
24. E, debunk. The other four mean to confirm or prove right. Debunk means to expose as false.
25. B, obtuse. The other four describe sharp judgment or wisdom. Obtuse means slow to understand.
