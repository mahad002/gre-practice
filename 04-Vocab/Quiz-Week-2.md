# Quiz: Week 2 (Days 6 to 10)

25 questions on the 100 words in `Words-Week-2.md`. Cover the answers at the bottom, write your answers down, then check them. Aim for 20 or more correct.

## Part A: Fill the blank

Choose the one word that best completes the sentence.

1. The new medication did little to ______ her pain; if anything, the side effects made her feel worse.

   (A) appropriate  (B) squander  (C) alleviate  (D) obfuscate  (E) exacerbate

2. Far from calming the dispute, the minister's careless remark ______ it, bringing thousands more protesters onto the streets.

   (A) exacerbated  (B) mollified  (C) husbanded  (D) tabled  (E) elucidated

3. The professor's explanation was so ______ that even first-year students understood the theory at once.

   (A) abstruse  (B) parsimonious  (C) haughty  (D) lucid  (E) nebulous

4. Too ______ to speak up in meetings, the talented analyst watched others take credit for her ideas.

   (A) imperious  (B) pompous  (C) diffident  (D) cogent  (E) lavish

5. The ______ heir ran through a fortune in three years, buying yachts he never sailed.

   (A) frugal  (B) meek  (C) austere  (D) explicit  (E) prodigal

6. The scientist was careful to ______ her claim, noting that the results applied only to mice and might not hold for humans.

   (A) champion  (B) qualify  (C) compound  (D) exact  (E) squander

7. With attendance low and several members absent, the council voted to ______ the motion until its next session.

   (A) table  (B) bolster  (C) placate  (D) tender  (E) manifest

8. In his ______, the champion refused to train, certain that no challenger could ever beat him.

   (A) avarice  (B) want  (C) spendthrift  (D) hubris  (E) buttress

9. Stranded for days, the sailors ______ their remaining water, allowing each man only a mouthful at dawn and dusk.

   (A) squandered  (B) husbanded  (C) tempered  (D) appropriated  (E) lavished

10. The contract's language was deliberately ______, so that neither party could be sure what had actually been promised.

   (A) equivocal  (B) unequivocal  (C) explicit  (D) coherent  (E) magnanimous

## Part B: Pick the two synonyms

Choose the two words that both fit the sentence and give it the same meaning.

11. The airline offered vouchers to ______ the passengers who had been stranded overnight.

   (A) aggravate  (B) placate  (C) squander  (D) obfuscate  (E) mollify  (F) undermine

12. The treatise is so ______ that only a few specialists claim to understand it.

   (A) meek  (B) explicit  (C) lucid  (D) esoteric  (E) opulent  (F) abstruse

13. The ______ waiter looked down his nose at anyone who ordered the cheapest wine.

   (A) haughty  (B) frugal  (C) diffident  (D) lucid  (E) supercilious  (F) unassuming

14. A ______ employer, he counted every paper clip and refused to replace a broken chair.

   (A) miserly  (B) parsimonious  (C) cogent  (D) affluent  (E) magnanimous  (F) munificent

15. Years of illness had ______ him, leaving him too weak to climb a single flight of stairs.

   (A) championed  (B) debilitated  (C) bolstered  (D) elucidated  (E) enervated  (F) appeased

16. The falsehood of his statement was ______ to everyone who had watched the recording.

   (A) demure  (B) nebulous  (C) manifest  (D) patent  (E) cryptic  (F) solvent

17. The palace was famous for its ______ halls, hung with silk and lined with gold.

   (A) meek  (B) cryptic  (C) opulent  (D) austere  (E) lavish  (F) indigent

18. She ______ her criticism with praise for the team's effort, so that no one left the meeting discouraged.

   (A) qualified  (B) tempered  (C) exacted  (D) aggravated  (E) compounded  (F) tabled

19. New witnesses ______ the prosecution's case, which now looked far stronger than it had a week before.

   (A) flagged  (B) tabled  (C) bolstered  (D) vitiated  (E) undermined  (F) buttressed

20. Her note was so ______ that we spent an hour trying to work out what she meant.

   (A) thrifty  (B) explicit  (C) cryptic  (D) unequivocal  (E) pompous  (F) enigmatic

## Part C: Which word does not belong?

Four of the five words are close in meaning. Choose the one that is not.

21. (A) alleviate  (B) ameliorate  (C) mitigate  (D) assuage  (E) aggravate

22. (A) supercilious  (B) imperious  (C) pompous  (D) meek  (E) haughty

23. (A) miserly  (B) profligate  (C) frugal  (D) thrifty  (E) parsimonious

24. (A) arcane  (B) abstruse  (C) explicit  (D) obscure  (E) opaque

25. (A) diffident  (B) unassuming  (C) demure  (D) pretentious  (E) self-effacing

## Answers

1. C, alleviate. The drug was expected to ease the pain but did little, so the blank means to lessen.
2. A, exacerbated. "Far from calming" signals the opposite: the remark made things worse.
3. D, lucid. Everyone understood at once, so the explanation was clear.
4. C, diffident. She lacks the confidence to speak, which is diffidence.
5. E, prodigal. Wasting a fortune on unused yachts is wasteful extravagance.
6. B, qualify. Adding limits to a statement is to qualify it.
7. A, table. "Until its next session" shows the motion was postponed.
8. D, hubris. Certainty that nobody can beat him is excessive pride.
9. B, husbanded. Rationing a mouthful at a time is using a resource sparingly.
10. A, equivocal. Language that leaves both parties unsure is open to two readings.
11. B and E, placate and mollify. Vouchers are meant to calm angry passengers.
12. D and F, esoteric and abstruse. Understood by only a few specialists means hard to understand.
13. A and E, haughty and supercilious. Looking down on others is haughty or supercilious behavior.
14. A and B, miserly and parsimonious. Counting paper clips shows extreme unwillingness to spend.
15. B and E, debilitated and enervated. "Too weak" shows he was drained of strength. Enervate means to weaken.
16. C and D, manifest and patent. Those who saw the recording could plainly see the lie, so it was obvious.
17. C and E, opulent and lavish. Silk and gold indicate rich luxury.
18. A and B, qualified and tempered. Softening criticism with praise is to temper or qualify it.
19. C and F, bolstered and buttressed. The case looks stronger, so the witnesses strengthened it.
20. C and F, cryptic and enigmatic. A note that takes an hour to interpret is puzzling.
21. E, aggravate. The other four mean to make better or less severe. Aggravate means to make worse.
22. D, meek. The other four describe pride or self-importance. Meek means gentle and submissive.
23. B, profligate. The other four describe careful or stingy spending. Profligate means recklessly wasteful.
24. C, explicit. The other four mean hard to understand. Explicit means clearly stated.
25. D, pretentious. The other four describe modesty or shyness. Pretentious means trying to seem important.
