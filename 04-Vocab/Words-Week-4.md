# Words: Week 4 (Days 16 to 20)

Words about quantity, obstacles, growth, comparison and mood.

## Day 16: Abundance and lack

| Word | Meaning | Example sentence | Close synonyms |
|---|---|---|---|
| copious | (adj) in large amounts | She took copious notes and filled three notebooks in one week. | profuse, abundant |
| profuse | (adj) given or produced in great quantity | He offered profuse apologies and said sorry at least ten times. | copious |
| plethora | (n) an amount that is more than enough | Shoppers face a plethora of choices, with fifty brands of cereal on one shelf. | surfeit, glut |
| surfeit | (n) an excessive amount | After a surfeit of rich food at the festival, nobody wanted dinner. | glut, plethora |
| glut | (n) an oversupply | A glut of oil on the market pushed prices to a ten-year low. | surfeit |
| replete | (adj) filled or well supplied with | The report was replete with errors, at least one on every page. | rife |
| rife | (adj) widespread, usually of something bad | The camp was rife with disease because there was no clean water. | prevalent, replete |
| ubiquitous | (adj) found everywhere | Smartphones are now ubiquitous, seen in every hand on every street. | pervasive |
| pervasive | (adj) spreading through every part | The smell of smoke was pervasive and clung to every room in the house. | ubiquitous, prevalent |
| prevalent | (adj) common at a particular time or place | Flu is most prevalent in winter, when clinics fill with patients. | rife, pervasive |
| myriad | (adj, n) countless; a very large number | The night sky showed myriad stars, far too many to count. | countless |
| superfluous | (adj) more than is needed; unnecessary | Cut the superfluous words, and your essay will be half as long and twice as clear. | extraneous, redundant |
| extraneous | (adj) not relevant or essential | The editor removed extraneous details that had nothing to do with the main story. | superfluous |
| dearth | (n) a lack or shortage | A dearth of qualified teachers forced the school to cancel its physics classes. | paucity, want |
| paucity | (n) smallness of number or amount | The paucity of evidence, a single blurred photo, made conviction impossible. | dearth |
| scant | (adj) barely enough; very little | He paid scant attention to the warning and soon regretted it. | meager, sparse |
| meager | (adj) small in amount and poor in quality | They survived on a meager diet of rice and water. | scant |
| sparse | (adj) thinly spread; not dense | Trees are sparse in the desert, with miles of sand between them. | scant |
| devoid | (adj) completely without | The landscape was devoid of life, with not even an insect to be seen. | bereft |
| bereft | (adj) deprived of; lacking, often with sadness | Bereft of hope after the third rejection, he stopped applying altogether. | devoid |

## Day 17: Blocking and helping

| Word | Meaning | Example sentence | Close synonyms |
|---|---|---|---|
| curtail | (v) to cut short or reduce | Budget cuts forced the library to curtail its opening hours. | truncate, reduce |
| constrain | (v) to limit or restrict | A lack of funds constrained the team, which could afford only one experiment. | inhibit, hamper |
| hamper | (v) to slow or make difficult | Heavy rain hampered the rescue effort and turned the roads to mud. | hinder, impede |
| hinder | (v) to delay or obstruct | A sprained ankle hindered her progress up the hill. | hamper, impede |
| impede | (v) to get in the way of | Fallen trees impeded the ambulance, which arrived an hour late. | hinder, hamper |
| stymie | (v) to block or frustrate completely | The detectives were stymied by a total lack of clues. | thwart |
| thwart | (v) to prevent from succeeding | Guards thwarted the escape by sealing the tunnel just in time. | stymie, foil |
| inhibit | (v) to hold back or restrain | Fear of ridicule inhibits many students from asking questions. | constrain |
| preclude | (v) to make impossible in advance | His injury precluded any chance of playing in the final. | forestall, obviate |
| forestall | (v) to prevent by acting first | The company forestalled a strike by raising wages before the union could vote. | preclude |
| obviate | (v) to remove the need for | Online banking obviates the need to visit a branch. | preclude |
| stifle | (v) to suppress or hold back | Strict rules stifled creativity, and the designers stopped suggesting new ideas. | quell, suppress |
| quell | (v) to put an end to, often by force; to calm | Troops were called in to quell the riot. | stifle, suppress |
| deter | (v) to discourage from acting | High fines deter drivers from speeding near schools. | discourage |
| encumber | (v) to weigh down or burden | Encumbered by heavy bags, she could not run for the bus. | hamper, burden |
| foster | (v) to encourage the growth of | Small classes foster close ties between teachers and pupils. | nurture, promote |
| facilitate | (v) to make easier | The new bridge facilitated trade between the two towns. | expedite |
| expedite | (v) to make happen sooner | Paying an extra fee will expedite your passport, which then arrives in two days. | facilitate |
| abet | (v) to help someone do wrong | He was charged with abetting the thief by lending him a car. | aid, assist |
| galvanize | (v) to shock or excite into action | The photo of the flooded village galvanized the public into donating millions. | spur |

## Day 18: Increase, decrease and spread

| Word | Meaning | Example sentence | Close synonyms |
|---|---|---|---|
| abate | (v) to become less intense | We waited indoors until the storm abated and the wind dropped. | subside, wane |
| subside | (v) to become calmer or lower | Her anger subsided once she heard the full explanation. | abate |
| wane | (v) to decrease gradually in size or strength | Interest in the series waned, and by the third season few people watched. | ebb, dwindle |
| ebb | (v, n) to flow away; to decline | His strength ebbed as the illness went on. | wane |
| dwindle | (v) to become steadily smaller | Their savings dwindled from thousands to almost nothing. | wane |
| attenuate | (v) to reduce the force or effect of | Thick walls attenuate the noise from the street. | weaken, mitigate |
| truncate | (v) to shorten by cutting off | The broadcast was truncated and ended ten minutes before the final scene. | curtail, abridge |
| abridge | (v) to shorten a text | The publisher abridged the novel from 900 pages to 300 for younger readers. | truncate |
| augment | (v) to increase by adding to | She augmented her salary by teaching evening classes. | amplify |
| amplify | (v) to make larger, louder or stronger | The microphone amplified her voice so that the back row could hear. | augment |
| proliferate | (v) to increase rapidly in number | Coffee shops proliferated until there was one on every corner. | multiply, burgeon |
| wax | (v) to grow larger or stronger (second meaning) | His enthusiasm waxed as the launch date came closer. | grow, swell |
| aggrandize | (v) to increase the power or importance of | He used the charity mainly to aggrandize himself and put his name on every building. | inflate, exalt |
| disseminate | (v) to spread widely, especially information | The agency disseminated the warning through radio, television and text messages. | propagate |
| propagate | (v) to spread; to reproduce | Social media helped propagate the rumor across the country in hours. | disseminate |
| diffuse | (v, adj) to spread over a wide area; spread out or wordy | The scent of the flowers diffused through the whole house. | permeate |
| inundate | (v) to flood; to overwhelm with too much | The office was inundated with complaints, thousands in a single day. | flood, overwhelm |
| permeate | (v) to spread through every part of | The smell of baking bread permeated the building. | diffuse, pervade |
| saturate | (v) to fill completely so that no more can be absorbed | Rain saturated the ground until water pooled on the surface. | soak |
| satiate | (v) to satisfy fully | The huge meal satiated even the hungriest of the hikers. | sate |

## Day 19: Similar and different

| Word | Meaning | Example sentence | Close synonyms |
|---|---|---|---|
| analogous | (adj) similar in a way that allows comparison | A computer's memory is analogous to a human brain's, though the two work differently. | akin |
| homogeneous | (adj) made of the same kind throughout | The village was homogeneous, as nearly everyone shared one language and religion. | uniform |
| heterogeneous | (adj) made of different kinds | The class was heterogeneous, with students aged from sixteen to sixty. | disparate, eclectic |
| disparate | (adj) fundamentally different | The book joins disparate subjects, from cooking to astrophysics, in one argument. | heterogeneous |
| discrepancy | (n) a difference between things that should match | Auditors found a discrepancy between the receipts and the recorded totals. | inconsistency |
| incongruous | (adj) out of place; not fitting | The glass tower looked incongruous among the old stone cottages. | out of place |
| dichotomy | (n) a division into two opposed parts | The essay rejects the dichotomy between work and play and argues that the two overlap. | split, contrast |
| diverge | (v) to move or develop in different directions | The twins' careers diverged, one becoming a surgeon and the other a sailor. | separate, differ |
| converge | (v) to come together from different directions | Thousands of fans converged on the stadium from every part of the city. | meet |
| commensurate | (adj) in proportion to | She wanted a salary commensurate with her twenty years of experience. | proportionate |
| tantamount | (adj) equal in effect to | Staying silent was tantamount to admitting guilt. | equivalent |
| amalgamate | (v) to combine into one | The two small banks amalgamated to form a single larger one. | merge |
| eclectic | (adj) drawn from many different sources | Her eclectic taste in music ranged from opera to hip-hop. | heterogeneous |
| dissonance | (n) lack of harmony or agreement | There was a dissonance between his kind words and his cold actions. | discord |
| akin | (adj) similar or related | Her feeling was akin to homesickness, though she had never lived there. | analogous |
| paradox | (n) a statement that seems contradictory but may be true | It is a paradox that saving more can make a whole economy poorer. | contradiction |
| antithesis | (n) the direct opposite | His quiet, careful style was the antithesis of his brother's loud showmanship. | opposite |
| juxtapose | (v) to place side by side for contrast | The exhibit juxtaposes photos of the street in 1900 and today. | set side by side |
| conflate | (v) to mix two things into one, often wrongly | The article conflates wealth with happiness, as if the two were the same. | confuse, amalgamate |
| disjointed | (adj) lacking order or connection | His disjointed speech jumped between topics with no clear thread. | incoherent |
