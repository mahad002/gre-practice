# Quiz: Week 5 (Days 21 to 25)

25 questions on the 100 words in `Words-Week-5.md`. Cover the answers at the bottom, write your answers down, then check them. Aim for 20 or more correct.

## Part A: Fill the blank

Choose the one word that best completes the sentence.

1. A lifelong ______, the old writer avoided all company and said that he preferred his dog to any human being.

   (A) sycophant  (B) misanthrope  (C) maverick  (D) iconoclast  (E) harbinger

2. The judge, known for her ______, could not be bribed or flattered into favoring either side.

   (A) impetus  (B) turpitude  (C) proclivity  (D) antipathy  (E) probity

3. The king was surrounded by ______ who praised his every decision, however foolish.

   (A) precursors  (B) sycophants  (C) misanthropes  (D) iconoclasts  (E) renegades

4. Their ______ meetings took place after midnight in an abandoned warehouse, far from any watching eyes.

   (A) salient  (B) auspicious  (C) conspicuous  (D) clandestine  (E) overt

5. The assassination of the archduke ______ a war that the great powers had long expected but none had planned for that summer.

   (A) precipitated  (B) flouted  (C) divulged  (D) exonerated  (E) cajoled

6. Security footage ______ the cashier, showing that she had been nowhere near the safe.

   (A) ingratiated  (B) exonerated  (C) fomented  (D) loathed  (E) insinuated

7. The ______ purpose of the trip was research, but the long days at the beach suggested otherwise.

   (A) ostensible  (B) prescient  (C) contrite  (D) tacit  (E) latent

8. After years in the emergency ward, the nurse had become ______ to sights that would make a newcomer faint.

   (A) enamored  (B) partisan  (C) subservient  (D) contrite  (E) inured

9. Motorists who ______ the new parking rules will have their cars removed.

   (A) flout  (B) augur  (C) absolve  (D) relish  (E) divulge

10. The economist's ______ article, published five years before the crash, described almost exactly how it would unfold.

   (A) prescient  (B) contrite  (C) fawning  (D) dormant  (E) venal

## Part B: Pick the two synonyms

Choose the two words that both fit the sentence and give it the same meaning.

11. The ______ assistant agreed with everything the director said and rushed to carry her bags.

   (A) impudent  (B) fawning  (C) latent  (D) obsequious  (E) insolent  (F) culpable

12. He has a ______ for exaggeration, so his friends halve every number he quotes.

   (A) proclivity  (B) propensity  (C) aversion  (D) anathema  (E) impetus  (F) antipathy

13. The ______ effects of the rumor spread slowly, and its damage was noticed only months later.

   (A) auspicious  (B) benign  (C) insidious  (D) benevolent  (E) overt  (F) pernicious

14. The signs were ______: with demand rising and costs falling, the launch could hardly have come at a better time.

   (A) furtive  (B) ominous  (C) flippant  (D) propitious  (E) auspicious  (F) nefarious

15. He took a ______ glance at his neighbor's answer sheet when the examiner turned away.

   (A) furtive  (B) benevolent  (C) conspicuous  (D) overt  (E) surreptitious  (F) contrite

16. The pupil's ______ reply to the head teacher earned him a week of detention.

   (A) altruistic  (B) insolent  (C) obsequious  (D) impudent  (E) tacit  (F) contrite

17. Rival leaders accused foreign agents of trying to ______ rebellion in the border provinces.

   (A) flout  (B) incite  (C) divulge  (D) absolve  (E) relish  (F) foment

18. Falling orders ______ a hard winter for the shipyards.

   (A) portended  (B) cajoled  (C) flouted  (D) divulged  (E) presaged  (F) absolved

19. She ______ cruelty to animals and would leave the room if anyone joked about it.

   (A) relished  (B) elicited  (C) loathed  (D) insinuated  (E) instigated  (F) abhorred

20. There was a ______ understanding between the neighbors, never put into words, that neither would build above two floors.

   (A) implicit  (B) nefarious  (C) tacit  (D) overt  (E) flagrant  (F) blatant

## Part C: Which word does not belong?

Four of the five words are close in meaning. Choose the one that is not.

21. (A) predilection  (B) propensity  (C) aversion  (D) proclivity  (E) penchant

22. (A) servile  (B) subservient  (C) insubordinate  (D) fawning  (E) obsequious

23. (A) furtive  (B) clandestine  (C) covert  (D) surreptitious  (E) overt

24. (A) depraved  (B) malevolent  (C) nefarious  (D) benevolent  (E) perfidious

25. (A) goad  (B) foment  (C) instigate  (D) absolve  (E) incite

## Answers

1. B, misanthrope. Someone who dislikes people in general is a misanthrope.
2. E, probity. Being impossible to bribe is complete honesty.
3. B, sycophants. People who praise even foolish decisions are flatterers.
4. D, clandestine. Meetings held at midnight away from watching eyes are secret.
5. A, precipitated. The event caused the war to break out suddenly.
6. B, exonerated. Proof that she was not there clears her of blame.
7. A, ostensible. The stated purpose seems not to be the real one.
8. E, inured. Long exposure has made her used to unpleasant sights.
9. A, flout. Cars are removed when drivers openly disobey the rules.
10. A, prescient. Describing the crash five years early is predicting events before they happen.
11. B and D, fawning and obsequious. Agreeing with everything and carrying bags shows too much eagerness to please. Insolent and impudent are opposites.
12. A and B, proclivity and propensity. He tends to exaggerate, so the blank means a natural tendency.
13. C and F, insidious and pernicious. Harm that spreads slowly and unnoticed is pernicious or insidious.
14. D and E, propitious and auspicious. "Could hardly have come at a better time" means the signs were favorable.
15. A and E, furtive and surreptitious. A glance taken when the examiner looks away is secret.
16. B and D, insolent and impudent. A reply that earns detention is rudely disrespectful.
17. B and F, incite and foment. Agents accused over a rebellion are said to stir it up.
18. A and E, portended and presaged. Falling orders are a sign of trouble to come.
19. C and F, loathed and abhorred. Leaving the room shows that she hated it.
20. A and C, implicit and tacit. An understanding never put into words is unstated.
21. C, aversion. The other four mean a liking or tendency toward something. Aversion means a strong dislike.
22. C, insubordinate. The other four mean too eager to obey or please. Insubordinate means refusing to obey.
23. E, overt. The other four mean secret. Overt means done openly.
24. D, benevolent. The other four describe wicked or treacherous character. Benevolent means kind.
25. D, absolve. The other four mean to stir up or provoke. Absolve means to free from guilt.
