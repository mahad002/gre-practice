# Quiz: Week 1 (Days 1 to 5)

25 questions on the 100 words in `Words-Week-1.md`. Cover the answers at the bottom, write your answers down, then check them. Aim for 20 or more correct.

## Part A: Fill the blank

Choose the one word that best completes the sentence.

1. Though the critics ______ the director's first film as a masterpiece, they dismissed his second as a failure.

   (A) lauded  (B) digressed  (C) feigned  (D) berated  (E) capitulated

2. The witness was so ______ that the lawyer had to drag each one-word answer out of him.

   (A) garrulous  (B) malleable  (C) effusive  (D) reticent  (E) capricious

3. Despite hours of pleading from both families, the landlord remained ______ and refused to delay the eviction by even a day.

   (A) obdurate  (B) candid  (C) fleeting  (D) pliable  (E) eloquent

4. The argument seemed convincing at first, but on closer inspection it proved ______, resting on a hidden error in logic.

   (A) artless  (B) specious  (C) perennial  (D) steadfast  (E) succinct

5. Fame gained through a viral video is usually ______; within a month the public has moved on.

   (A) dogged  (B) verbose  (C) ephemeral  (D) immutable  (E) entrenched

6. Rather than give a direct answer, the spokesman ______, using phrases vague enough to satisfy both sides.

   (A) fluctuated  (B) acquiesced  (C) equivocated  (D) extolled  (E) upbraided

7. Because clay is ______ when wet, a potter can shape it into almost any form.

   (A) malleable  (B) terse  (C) volatile  (D) obstinate  (E) mendacious

8. The editor asked for a ______ summary, no longer than three sentences, of the 200-page report.

   (A) bombastic  (B) mutable  (C) succinct  (D) prolix  (E) spurious

9. After a month-long strike that emptied its warehouses, the company finally ______ and accepted every one of the union's demands.

   (A) vacillated  (B) disparaged  (C) capitulated  (D) dissembled  (E) digressed

10. The laws of arithmetic are ______: two plus two will equal four in every country and every century.

   (A) immutable  (B) transitory  (C) disingenuous  (D) fickle  (E) curt

## Part B: Pick the two synonyms

Choose the two words that both fit the sentence and give it the same meaning.

11. The senator's ______ speech ran for three hours and sent half the chamber to sleep.

   (A) pithy  (B) verbose  (C) tenacious  (D) prolix  (E) erratic  (F) candid

12. Investors avoid the currency because its value is so ______, rising and falling sharply from one day to the next.

   (A) static  (B) laconic  (C) erratic  (D) volatile  (E) artless  (F) steadfast

13. The reviewer ______ the novel, calling it clumsy, dull and dishonest.

   (A) venerated  (B) castigated  (C) conceded  (D) extolled  (E) lambasted  (F) feigned

14. Her ______ manner was a contrast to the scheming of her colleagues; she said exactly what she thought and hid nothing.

   (A) garrulous  (B) dogmatic  (C) artless  (D) guileless  (E) mendacious  (F) whimsical

15. Negotiations failed because the union leader was ______, rejecting every compromise that was offered.

   (A) amenable  (B) eloquent  (C) fleeting  (D) obstinate  (E) compliant  (F) intransigent

16. A ______ man, the lighthouse keeper could pass a whole week without speaking ten words.

   (A) taciturn  (B) forthright  (C) fickle  (D) voluble  (E) loquacious  (F) laconic

17. The children ______ the old storyteller, treating her every word as precious.

   (A) fluctuated  (B) feigned  (C) revered  (D) derided  (E) belittled  (F) venerated

18. The committee ______ for months between the two designs, favoring one on Monday and the other by Friday.

   (A) digressed  (B) wavered  (C) capitulated  (D) dissembled  (E) acquiesced  (F) vacillated

19. The reasoning behind the proposal was ______: it looked sound but collapsed under the first serious question.

   (A) abiding  (B) fallacious  (C) candid  (D) specious  (E) scrupulous  (F) concise

20. The relief was ______; within an hour the pain had returned.

   (A) perennial  (B) abiding  (C) fleeting  (D) obstinate  (E) transient  (F) verbose

## Part C: Which word does not belong?

Four of the five words are close in meaning. Choose the one that is not.

21. (A) voluble  (B) terse  (C) verbose  (D) garrulous  (E) loquacious

22. (A) rebuke  (B) extol  (C) censure  (D) chastise  (E) upbraid

23. (A) amenable  (B) pliable  (C) tractable  (D) docile  (E) adamant

24. (A) feign  (B) acquiesce  (C) fabricate  (D) prevaricate  (E) dissemble

25. (A) whimsical  (B) mercurial  (C) steadfast  (D) fickle  (E) capricious

## Answers

1. A, lauded. "As a masterpiece" needs a word of praise, and "though" sets it against the later dismissal.
2. D, reticent. One-word answers that must be dragged out show unwillingness to speak.
3. A, obdurate. He is unmoved by pleading, which is the core of obdurate.
4. B, specious. Specious means looking sound while actually being false.
5. C, ephemeral. The public moves on within a month, so the fame is very short-lived.
6. C, equivocated. Deliberately vague language used to avoid commitment is equivocation.
7. A, malleable. Something that can be shaped into any form is malleable.
8. C, succinct. Three sentences for 200 pages means brief and clear.
9. C, capitulated. Accepting every demand after resisting is surrender.
10. A, immutable. Something true everywhere and always cannot change.
11. B and D, verbose and prolix. Both mean wordy and too long. Pithy is the opposite.
12. C and D, erratic and volatile. Sharp daily rises and falls are unpredictable change.
13. B and E, castigated and lambasted. Both mean to criticize harshly. Extolled and venerated are praise.
14. C and D, artless and guileless. The contrast with scheming calls for words meaning without deceit.
15. D and F, obstinate and intransigent. Rejecting every compromise is stubbornness. Amenable and compliant are opposites.
16. A and F, taciturn and laconic. Both describe someone of very few words. Voluble and loquacious are a pair with the opposite meaning.
17. C and F, revered and venerated. Treating every word as precious shows deep respect.
18. B and F, wavered and vacillated. Switching back and forth between two choices is vacillating or wavering.
19. B and D, fallacious and specious. Reasoning that looks sound but is wrong is specious or fallacious.
20. C and E, fleeting and transient. Relief gone within an hour is short-lived. Perennial and abiding mean long-lasting.
21. B, terse. The other four mean talkative or wordy. Terse means brief.
22. B, extol. The other four mean to criticize or scold. Extol means to praise.
23. E, adamant. The other four mean easy to influence or control. Adamant means refusing to be persuaded.
24. B, acquiesce. The other four involve deceiving or hiding the truth. Acquiesce means to accept without protest.
25. C, steadfast. The other four mean changeable. Steadfast means firm and unchanging.
