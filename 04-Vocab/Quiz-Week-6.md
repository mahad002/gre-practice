# Quiz: Week 6 (Days 26 to 30)

25 questions on the 100 words in `Words-Week-6.md`. Cover the answers at the bottom, write your answers down, then check them. Aim for 20 or more correct.

## Part A: Fill the blank

Choose the one word that best completes the sentence.

1. The lecture grew so long that the audience became ______, shifting in their seats and whispering.

   (A) intrepid  (B) disinterested  (C) pristine  (D) restive  (E) urbane

2. A referee must be ______, with no reason to prefer one team over the other.

   (A) officious  (B) craven  (C) gaudy  (D) ambivalent  (E) disinterested

3. A ______ smell from the rotting fish drove the visitors out of the market.

   (A) noisome  (B) apposite  (C) salubrious  (D) pristine  (E) ornate

4. The path up the cliff was ______, doubling back on itself more than thirty times.

   (A) tortuous  (B) glib  (C) decorous  (D) venial  (E) fulsome

5. He had the ______ to correct the surgeon in the middle of the operation, though he had been a student for only a week.

   (A) trepidation  (B) temerity  (C) opprobrium  (D) efficacy  (E) propriety

6. The law ______ the sale of fireworks to anyone under eighteen, and shops that ignore it are fined.

   (A) proscribes  (B) avers  (C) recants  (D) sullies  (E) peruses

7. Although the spider looks dangerous, it is entirely ______ and can safely be picked up.

   (A) deleterious  (B) formidable  (C) noxious  (D) innocuous  (E) virulent

8. Her praise was so ______ that the author suspected she had not read the book at all.

   (A) viable  (B) spartan  (C) fulsome  (D) venial  (E) stark

9. Finding that the experiment had been contaminated, the scientist publicly ______ the claim she had made the year before.

   (A) perused  (B) averred  (C) daunted  (D) adulterated  (E) recanted

10. The essay offered a ______ critique that went straight to the weakest point of the theory.

   (A) fortuitous  (B) timorous  (C) florid  (D) glib  (E) trenchant

## Part B: Pick the two synonyms

Choose the two words that both fit the sentence and give it the same meaning.

11. Smoking has ______ effects on almost every organ of the body, which is why doctors urge patients to quit.

   (A) deleterious  (B) detrimental  (C) apposite  (D) salubrious  (E) venial  (F) salutary

12. The ______ deserter fled at the first sound of gunfire, leaving his comrades behind.

   (A) pusillanimous  (B) circumspect  (C) craven  (D) pristine  (E) trenchant  (F) suave

13. The ______ explorer crossed the ice alone, undeterred by storms or hunger.

   (A) ornate  (B) timorous  (C) intrepid  (D) glib  (E) apprehensive  (F) dauntless

14. His ______ review mocked the play so bitterly that the theater canceled its run.

   (A) innocuous  (B) decorous  (C) caustic  (D) understated  (E) fulsome  (F) acerbic

15. The ______ sign, flashing pink and green, could be seen from three streets away.

   (A) garish  (B) spartan  (C) stark  (D) pristine  (E) gaudy  (F) understated

16. His ______ behavior at dinner, shouting across the table and insulting the cook, embarrassed his hosts.

   (A) boorish  (B) wary  (C) urbane  (D) uncouth  (E) suave  (F) decorous

17. The march through the swamp was ______, and several soldiers collapsed from exhaustion.

   (A) arduous  (B) innocuous  (C) fortuitous  (D) salubrious  (E) pristine  (F) laborious

18. Once deceived by a partner, she was ______ of signing any agreement without a lawyer present.

   (A) brazen  (B) wary  (C) intrepid  (D) fulsome  (E) chary  (F) officious

19. The coach's half-time ______ against lazy defending could be heard outside the stadium.

   (A) tirade  (B) panegyric  (C) euphemism  (D) harangue  (E) eulogy  (F) qualm

20. Engineers concluded that a tunnel under the bay was not ______, since the rock was too soft to hold.

   (A) viable  (B) feasible  (C) futile  (D) cumbersome  (E) arduous  (F) innocuous

## Part C: Which word does not belong?

Four of the five words are close in meaning. Choose the one that is not.

21. (A) detrimental  (B) noxious  (C) virulent  (D) deleterious  (E) salubrious

22. (A) audacious  (B) timorous  (C) dauntless  (D) intrepid  (E) brazen

23. (A) ostentatious  (B) understated  (C) garish  (D) gaudy  (E) tawdry

24. (A) panegyric  (B) diatribe  (C) invective  (D) tirade  (E) harangue

25. (A) crass  (B) urbane  (C) uncouth  (D) churlish  (E) boorish

## Answers

1. D, restive. Shifting and whispering show restlessness. Disinterested means impartial, not bored.
2. E, disinterested. Having no reason to prefer either side is being impartial.
3. A, noisome. Rotting fish produce a foul smell, which is what noisome means.
4. A, tortuous. A path that doubles back many times is full of twists.
5. B, temerity. A new student correcting a surgeon shows reckless boldness.
6. A, proscribes. Shops are fined, so the law forbids the sale.
7. D, innocuous. "Although" and "safely" show that the spider is harmless.
8. C, fulsome. Praise so excessive that it seems insincere is fulsome.
9. E, recanted. Withdrawing an earlier claim is recanting it.
10. E, trenchant. A critique that goes straight to the weak point is sharp and forceful.
11. A and B, deleterious and detrimental. Doctors urge quitting because the effects are harmful. Salubrious and salutary mean beneficial.
12. A and C, pusillanimous and craven. Fleeing and abandoning comrades is cowardice.
13. C and F, intrepid and dauntless. Undeterred by storms or hunger means fearless.
14. C and F, caustic and acerbic. Bitter mockery is caustic or acerbic.
15. A and E, garish and gaudy. Flashing pink and green is unpleasantly bright and showy.
16. A and D, boorish and uncouth. Shouting and insulting the cook is rude and ill-mannered. Urbane and suave are opposites.
17. A and F, arduous and laborious. Soldiers collapsing from exhaustion shows that it needed great effort.
18. B and E, wary and chary. Past deception has made her cautious.
19. A and D, tirade and harangue. A loud speech against something is an angry attack. Eulogy and panegyric are speeches of praise.
20. A and B, viable and feasible. Rock too soft to hold means the tunnel cannot be built.
21. E, salubrious. The other four mean harmful. Salubrious means good for health.
22. B, timorous. The other four mean bold or fearless. Timorous means easily frightened.
23. B, understated. The other four mean showy. Understated means quiet and restrained.
24. A, panegyric. The other four are verbal attacks. A panegyric is a speech of praise.
25. B, urbane. The other four mean rude or crude. Urbane means polished and well-mannered.
